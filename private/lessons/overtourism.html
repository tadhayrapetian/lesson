Background: In 2024, residents protested against mass tourism in Barcelona, the Canary Islands and other places. Venice introduced a fee for day visitors on busy days in 2024. Barcelona plans to end tourist apartment licences by 2028.

1

Warm-up

6 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. What is the most crowded tourist place you have ever visited? How did it feel?
  2. Is tourism good for your city or country? Why?
  3. Would you pay extra to visit a famous place if it meant fewer crowds?

Teacher notes. 6 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Think about the last time you visited a busy tourist place. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Give students 1 minute to think, then pairs discuss. Take two or three ideas from the class. Do not correct language here. This stage is about interest and ideas.

Say: Great ideas. Today’s text has some useful words for this topic. Let’s look at them first.

3

First read

6 min

Read the text quickly. Choose the best summary.

When Tourists Become the Problem

From Venice to Barcelona, local people say enough is enough. Can cities welcome visitors and still be places to live?

In the summer of 2024, residents of Barcelona did something unusual. They walked through tourist areas with water pistols and gently sprayed visitors, holding signs that said ‘Tourists go home’. It was a protest, not an attack, but the message was clear. Many residents feel that their city has been taken over.

The word for this is overtourism. Cheap flights, cruise ships and social media have made famous places more overcrowded than ever. In some old city centres, there are more visitors than locals on a summer day. Streets are blocked, beaches are full, and traditional shops are replaced by souvenir stands.

Housing is the biggest complaint. Thousands of apartments in popular cities are now used as short-term rentals for tourists. This has driven up rents, and many young locals can no longer afford to live in the neighbourhoods where they grew up. Barcelona has announced that its tourist apartment licences will not be renewed after 2028.

Other places are trying different solutions. Since 2024, day-trippers to Venice have been charged a fee on busy days. Amsterdam has raised its tourist tax and stopped new hotels from opening in the centre. Some popular sites cap the number of visitors per day, so tickets must be booked weeks in advance.

Not everyone agrees with these measures. Tourism creates millions of jobs, and some business owners fear that a backlash against visitors will hurt the economy. Experts suggest a middle way: spreading tourists more evenly through the year and encouraging travellers to go off the beaten track. ‘The question isn’t whether people should travel,’ says one tourism researcher. ‘It’s how.’

Teacher notes. 6 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Don’t stop at new words. Choose the best summary. You have 3 minutes.

ICQs: “Do you need to understand every word?” (No.) “How many answers do you choose?” (One.)

Set a strict time limit. Pairs compare. In feedback ask: “Which part of the text helped you?”

Answer: B. Problems in paragraphs 1 to 3, solutions in paragraph 4, a balanced view in paragraph 5.

Say: Now read again, more carefully this time.

4

Close read

10 min

Read again. Are the sentences true (T), false (F) or not given (NG)?

  • The Barcelona protesters attacked tourists violently.
  • In some city centres, visitors can outnumber locals in summer.
  • Short-term rentals have made housing cheaper for locals.
  • Venice charges all visitors a fee every day of the year.
  • Most residents of Venice support the fee.
  • Some business owners are worried about the economy.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read again carefully. Are the sentences true (T), false (F) or not given (NG)? Underline the part of the text that gives you the answer.

ICQ: “What does not given mean?” (The text doesn’t say.)

Pairs compare and show each other the evidence. In feedback, always ask “Where does it say that?”

Say: Well done. Now let’s look closely at some language from the text.

5

Language focus

12 min

Look at these sentences from the text.

  • Many residents feel that their city has been taken over.
  • ...and traditional shops are replaced by souvenir stands.
  • Since 2024, day-trippers to Venice have been charged a fee on busy days.
  • ...so tickets must be booked weeks in advance.
  • Barcelona has announced that its tourist apartment licences will not be renewed after 2028.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • In ‘Traditional shops are replaced by souvenir stands’, who or what does the action?The souvenir stands (after by). The focus is on the shops.
  • How do we form the passive?be in the correct tense + past participle.
  • Why does the writer say ‘tickets must be booked’ and not ‘visitors must book tickets’?The focus is on the tickets and the rule. Who books them is obvious.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Focus: the passive, including modal passives.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Work with a partner and answer the questions. Don’t worry about rules yet. Just look for patterns.

Monitor. Then elicit answers and build the rule on the board with the students. Students tell you the rule. You write it.

Rule for the board: Passive = be + past participle. Present: is / are done. Present perfect: has / have been done. Future: will be done. Modal: must / should / can be done. Continuous: is being done. Use it when the action is more important than the doer, when the doer is unknown or obvious, and in news and formal writing. Add by + agent only when it adds useful information.

CCQs: ‘The museum has been closed.’ Do we know who closed it? (No.) Is that important? (Not really.) Is it closed now? (Yes.)

Students often forget be: ‘Tickets must booked.’ Or use a wrong participle: ‘has been builded’.

Say: Now let’s practise it.

6

Practice

8 min

Rewrite the sentences in the passive. Use the word given.

  • The city council has banned cruise ships from the centre.beenCruise ships have been banned from the centre (by the city council).
  • Visitors must book tickets online.bookedTickets must be booked online.
  • The government will introduce a new tourist tax next year.introducedA new tourist tax will be introduced next year.
  • People are turning flats into holiday rentals.beingFlats are being turned into holiday rentals.
  • They should limit the number of visitors.limitedThe number of visitors should be limited.
  • Locals don’t welcome large tour groups.welcomedLarge tour groups aren’t welcomed by locals.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Work alone first. Complete the sentences. Then check with your partner.

ICQs: ‘Alone or with a partner first?’ (Alone.) ‘Do you always need by?’ (No. Only when it’s important.)

Fast finishers write one more sentence of their own with the target language.

In feedback, write the answers on the board. Focus on form errors. Ask “Why this form?” for one or two items.

Say: Perfect. Now you’re ready to use all of this in a real conversation.

7

Speaking

16 min

The town hall meeting

Santa Elena is a small, beautiful seaside town. In summer it gets 20,000 visitors a day, and only 6,000 people live there. Tonight the town decides what to do.

The mayor

You chair the meeting. You want a solution that keeps both residents and businesses happy.

A hotel owner

Tourism is your whole business. You are worried about any limits on visitors.

A young resident

You grew up here, but you can’t afford the rent. Most flats on your street are holiday rentals.

An environmental group

The beaches are polluted and the water supply is under pressure in summer.

A café owner

You need tourists in summer, but the crowds drive your regular local customers away.

The five proposals

1. A €10 fee for day-trippers. 2. A limit of 8,000 visitors a day. 3. No new holiday rentals. 4. A ban on cruise ships. 5. Advertising to bring visitors in the quiet winter season.

Steps

  1. Read your role and the five proposals.
  2. Decide which proposals you support and which you oppose, and why.
  3. Hold the meeting. The mayor gives everyone a turn.
  4. Vote. You can choose a maximum of two proposals.
  5. Report the decision: It has been decided that... Cruise ships will be...

Useful language

From my point of view, ...This should be introduced because...That would hurt...Could we find a compromise?I’m strongly against...It has been agreed that...

Teacher notes. 16 min. Pairs or small groups.

Say: Tonight there’s a town hall meeting in Santa Elena. Read your role card and the five proposals. You have three minutes to prepare your arguments.

Groups of four to six, one role each. In smaller classes, combine roles. 3 min preparation, 10 min meeting, 3 min reporting. The mayor chairs. Insist on the passive in the final report.

Give clear preparation time before students speak. Monitor closely. Write down good language and errors in two columns. Do not interrupt. Fluency comes first here.

One-to-one: you take the other role or position. Keep your turns short and push the student to justify and expand (“Why?”, “Can you give an example?”).

Say: Time’s up. Let’s look at some of the language you used.

8

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today's lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board: some correct, some with errors. Do not say who said them.

Say: Here are some sentences I heard. Some are perfect. Some have a mistake. Work with your partner and find the mistakes.

Students correct in pairs. Elicit the corrections. Praise good language and highlight two or three great phrases students used.

Errors students often make with this lesson:

  • “The city is visiting by millions of tourists.” > “The city is visited by millions of tourists.”
  • “Tourists must to pay a tax.” > “Tourists must pay a tax.”
  • “The prices have raised a lot.” > “The prices have risen a lot.”

Then students complete the reflection boxes. Ask one or two students to read theirs aloud.

Homework

Set at the end

A. Vocabulary review. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson. Change the form if you need to.

capoff the beaten trackresidentsovercrowdedbacklash
  • The beach was so overcrowded that we couldn’t find a place to sit.
  • Local residents complained about the noise from the bars.
  • Some cities cap the number of cruise ships that can arrive each day.
  • The new hotel caused a backlash from people who live nearby.
  • We avoided the famous sights and went off the beaten track.

B. Writing: a letter to a newspaper (180-220 words)

Your town or city is becoming very popular with tourists. Write a letter to a local newspaper. Describe two problems and suggest two solutions. Explain how they would help both locals and visitors.

Checklist

  • Use the passive at least four times, including one modal passive.
  • Use five words from the lesson.
  • Write in a formal style, without contractions.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and Part B. In Part B, use at least four words from today and the grammar we studied.

Collect the writing next lesson. Mark it for four things: task completion, vocabulary from the lesson, accuracy with the target language, and organisation.

Look for a formal register: no contractions, clear paragraphs, a polite closing.

Start the next lesson with a two-minute vocabulary quiz using the words from Part A.
