The Tipping Point

Payment screens now ask for a tip almost everywhere. Did you have to pay that? Should you have?

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • talk about past obligation, necessity and expectations
  • use needn’t have, didn’t have to, should have and was supposed to
  • describe travel mistakes and give advice

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Grammar. Length: 75 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Grammar: past modals and semi-modals of obligation, necessity and regret.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for detail. Speaking: writing a tipping guide for tourists.

Language aim: had to, didn’t have to / didn’t need to, needn’t have + past participle, should(n’t) have, was supposed to.

Background: ‘Tipflation’ describes tips being expected in more places and at higher percentages, especially in the US, where many service workers earn a low base wage. The lesson follows a Test-Teach-Test structure.

1

Warm-up

6 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. Do people tip in your country? Where? How much?
  2. Have you ever felt pressure to leave a tip?
  3. Should tips be included in the price? Why or why not?

Teacher notes. 6 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Let’s talk about tips. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Note: tipping norms differ in Armenia and Russia. 10% in restaurants is common, and service charges are often included. Use this for contrast.

  • You left such a big tip. It was too much!
  • I pay for the water. It was free.
  • We booked a table. The restaurant was empty.
  • You tip the waiter, not the chef.

3

Read

10 min

Confessions of a Confused Tipper

Last month I went to New York for the first time. I had read about tipping, but nothing prepared me for the reality.

On my first morning, I bought a coffee to take away. The barista turned a screen towards me with three buttons: 18%, 20% and 25%. I panicked and pressed 25%. I didn’t have to tip at all for a takeaway coffee, a friend told me later, but I felt I was supposed to.

That evening, I had dinner in a restaurant. Here I really did have to tip: waiters in the US often earn a very low basic wage and depend on tips. I left 15%, which I thought was generous. The waiter looked disappointed. Apparently, 20% is now normal. I should have checked before I went.

On the last day, I added a big tip to a restaurant bill, and then noticed that a ‘service charge’ had already been included. I needn’t have tipped twice! I felt silly, and poorer.

Back home, I’ve been thinking about it. Americans call this ‘tipflation’: tips are expected in more places, and the percentages keep rising. Some people say it’s unfair to customers. Others say workers need the money because wages are too low. Personally, I wish prices simply included everything. But until they do, I’ll be checking every bill.

Are the sentences true (T), false (F) or not given (NG)?

  • The writer had never been to New York before.
  • The writer’s friend said a tip was necessary for a takeaway coffee.
  • The waiter was happy with a 15% tip.
  • The writer tipped twice on the last day.
  • The writer’s friend lives in New York.
  • The writer thinks prices should include everything.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the blog post. True, false or not given? You have five minutes.

Ask: ‘Has anything like this happened to you?’ Take one or two stories.

4

Language focus

14 min

Look at these examples.

  • I didn’t have to tip at all for a takeaway coffee...
  • ...but I felt I was supposed to.
  • Here I really did have to tip...
  • I should have checked before I went.
  • I needn’t have tipped twice!

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Compare ‘I didn’t have to tip’ and ‘I needn’t have tipped’. Which one tells us that the writer actually tipped?needn’t have tipped: she tipped, but it wasn’t necessary. didn’t have to: it wasn’t necessary. The structure alone doesn’t tell us if she did it.
  • What does ‘I should have checked’ mean? Did the writer check?No, she didn’t check. It expresses regret: it was a good idea, but she didn’t do it.
  • What does ‘was supposed to’ express?An expectation or a rule: people expect you to do it.

Rule for the board: past obligation, necessity and regret

had to + verb = it was necessary. didn’t have to / didn’t need to + verb = it wasn’t necessary. needn’t have + past participle = you did it, but it wasn’t necessary. should have / shouldn’t have + past participle = regret or criticism. was / were supposed to + verb = it was expected or arranged, and often it didn’t happen. Emphatic did: I did have to tip.

CCQs: ‘We needn’t have taken a taxi.’ Did we take one? (Yes.) Was it necessary? (No.) ‘We didn’t need to take a taxi, so we walked.’ Did we take one? (No.)

Common mistakes: ‘I must have paid’ when students mean ‘I had to pay’ (must have = deduction). Also ‘needn’t to have’.

Teacher notes. 14 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Answer the questions with your partner.

Use two quick timelines on the board: one for needn’t have (action happened, X necessary) and one for should have (action didn’t happen, but it was a good idea).

Say: Now go back to stage 2 and check your answers.

Practice: choose

8 min

Choose the correct option.

  • The museum was free, so we pay.
  • We brought so much food. Nobody was hungry.
  • I’m sorry. I told you about the service charge.
  • My flight was cancelled, so I stay another night.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Choose the correct option. Look at the verb form after the gap. It helps you.

Feedback: ask ‘Did it happen?’ for each sentence.

6

Practice: rewrite

8 min

Rewrite the sentences with the word given.

  • It was necessary for us to wait for an hour.hadWe had to wait for an hour.
  • I took an umbrella, but it didn’t rain.needn’tI needn’t have taken an umbrella.
  • It was a mistake not to read the reviews.shouldI should have read the reviews.
  • It wasn’t necessary to book. There were lots of free tables.didn’tWe didn’t have to book. / We didn’t need to book.
  • It was a mistake to order the fish.shouldn’tI shouldn’t have ordered the fish.
  • The plan was to meet at 8, but Ana was late.supposedWe were supposed to meet at 8, but Ana was late.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Rewrite the sentences using the word given. Keep the meaning.

Monitor for ‘needn’t have + past participle’.

8

Speaking: the tipping guide

12 min

Write a tipping guide for tourists visiting your country. First, decide what to do in these situations.

The taxi

The driver helped you carry three heavy bags.

The self-service screen

You bought a bottle of water from a machine. The screen asks for 20%.

The hairdresser

You had a great haircut, and it took an hour.

The hotel room

You stayed for a week, and the room was cleaned every day.

The delivery

Your food arrived in heavy rain, ten minutes early.

Steps

  1. For each card, decide: tip or no tip? How much?
  2. Share stories of tipping mistakes. Use had to, needn’t have and should have.
  3. Write five rules for tourists visiting your country.
  4. Compare your rules with another group.

Useful language

You don’t have to tip...Tourists are supposed to...I once had to...I needn’t have...I should have asked...In our country, it’s normal to...

Teacher notes. 12 min. Groups of three or four.

Say: You’re writing a tipping guide for tourists. First, decide what to do in each situation.

Groups. 2 min reading, 6 min discussion, 4 min writing rules and comparing.

Interesting cultural point: a tip can be rude in Japan. Use it if students say ‘Everyone tips everywhere’.

9

Reflection and feedback

3 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 3 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘I must have paid a tax at the airport.’ > ‘I had to pay a tax at the airport.’
  • ‘We needn’t to have booked.’ > ‘We needn’t have booked.’
  • ‘I should checked the bill.’ > ‘I should have checked the bill.’
10

Homework

Set at the end

A. Complete the sentences with the correct form of the words in brackets.

  • We needn’t have taken (not need / take) a taxi. The hotel was only five minutes away, but we didn’t know.
  • I had to (have to) show my passport at the hotel.
  • You should have told (should / tell) me it was your birthday!
  • We were supposed to (be supposed to) check out at 11, but we left at 12.
  • He shouldn’t have left (shouldn’t / leave) his bag on the train.

Write a short story (150-180 words).

Write about a trip, meal or evening when something went wrong. What did you have to do? What should you have done? What needn’t you have worried about?

Checklist

  • Use at least four past modals from the lesson.
  • Use paragraphs: beginning, problem, ending.
  • End with a lesson you learned.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, complete Part A and write the story.

Mark the story for accurate past modals and narrative organisation.
