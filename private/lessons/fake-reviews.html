Five Stars, Zero Truth

Can you trust online reviews? Learn the words to describe products and services honestly.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • use words for reviews, shopping and customer service
  • use strong adjectives with absolutely, and normal adjectives with very
  • write and spot honest and fake reviews

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Vocabulary. Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Vocabulary: review and shopping words, strong adjectives, review expressions.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for detail. Speaking: a review guessing game.

Language aim: very + normal adjectives, absolutely + strong adjectives. Fixed expressions such as live up to the hype.

3

Read

9 min

The Fake Review Factory

Five stars? Great. Two stars? No, thanks. But how many of those reviews are genuine?

When you choose a restaurant or a hotel, you probably look at the rating first. But experts think that a large number of online reviews are fake. Some companies pay people to write excellent reviews of their own products. Others write terrible reviews of their competitors. And now AI can write hundreds of reviews in seconds, and they sound very real.

Governments are fighting back. Fake reviews are now illegal in the UK, and the US also has strict new rules.

How can you spot a fake? Be careful with reviews that are all extreme: ‘absolutely amazing!’ or ‘totally awful!’. Real customers usually write about details: the delivery was slow, the room was tiny, but the customer service was helpful. And if a product gets hundreds of five-star reviews on the same day, something is wrong.

Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • The writer thinks people often look at the rating first.
  • Some companies write bad reviews about other companies.
  • AI reviews are easy to recognise.
  • Fake reviews are illegal in the UK.
  • Genuine reviews often include small details.
  • Many five-star reviews on the same day are a good sign.

Teacher notes. 9 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text. True or false? You have four minutes.

Pairs compare. Ask for evidence.

Say: The text says ‘absolutely amazing’ and ‘tiny’. Let’s look at strong adjectives.

Say: Read the four expressions and their meanings. Then complete the sentences.

ICQ: ‘Can you change the form?’ (Yes. Look at item 2.)

Ask students for one real example for each expression.

6

Speaking: true or fake?

13 min

Write two short reviews of places you know. One is true. One is fake. Can your partner spot the fake?

Steps

  1. Think of a café, shop, hotel or app you know.
  2. Write two short reviews, three or four sentences each: one true and one invented. Use strong adjectives and review expressions.
  3. Read your reviews to your partner. Your partner asks three questions and guesses which one is fake.
  4. Discuss: should apps show who wrote each review? Would you trust reviews more?

Useful language

It was absolutely...The staff went out of their way to...It’s great value for money.It didn’t live up to the hype.I think the second one is fake because...What did you order?

Teacher notes. 13 min. Pairs.

Say: Write two short reviews. One true, one invented. Make the fake one hard to spot! You have four minutes.

Pairs swap and guess. Change partners and repeat if there is time.

One-to-one: you write two reviews too. The student guesses yours.

7

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘very delicious’ > ‘absolutely delicious’ or ‘really delicious’
  • ‘The service was very well.’ > ‘The service was very good.’
  • ‘I want to return my money.’ > ‘I want a refund.’
8

Homework

Set at the end

A. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson.

fakeratingoverpricedcustomer servicerefund
  • The hotel gave me a full refund because the room was not clean.
  • Twenty euros for a sandwich? It’s really overpriced.
  • I called customer service three times, but nobody answered.
  • This bag looks like a designer bag, but it’s fake.
  • The restaurant has a 4.8 rating on Google Maps.

Write an honest review (100-120 words).

Write a review of a product, place or service you used recently. Describe the good and bad points and give it a rating from one to five stars.

Checklist

  • Use at least three strong adjectives with absolutely or really.
  • Use two review expressions from stage 5.
  • Give a clear final rating and explain it.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and write a real review.

Encourage students to post their review online in English if they want a real reader.
