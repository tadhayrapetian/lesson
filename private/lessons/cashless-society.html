Do You Still Carry Cash?

Cards, phones and QR codes are everywhere. Learn to give opinions and reasons in a real discussion.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • give opinions with reasons and examples
  • agree, disagree politely and interrupt
  • take part in a role-play meeting about a real issue

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Speaking. Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Speaking: fluency and functional language in a discussion and a role-play meeting.

Subsidiary aims: Reading a model dialogue. Pronunciation: polite intonation. Vocabulary for payments.

Language aim: Functional language: opinions, reasons, agreeing, disagreeing and interrupting.

Background: Card and phone payments have grown fast everywhere. Some countries, such as Sweden, use very little cash. Several countries and cities have rules that protect people’s right to pay in cash.

3

Model discussion

8 min

Cash or no cash?

Ana and Leo are friends. Read their discussion.

Ana: In my opinion, cash will disappear in ten years. I never use it. I just tap my phone.

Leo: I’m not so sure. What about elderly people? My grandmother doesn’t have a smartphone.

Ana: That’s a good point. But young people prefer cards. For example, my friends never carry coins.

Leo: Maybe, but cash helps me keep track of my money. When I see the banknotes leave my wallet, I spend less.

Ana: Sorry to interrupt, but you can check a banking app too.

Leo: True, but it doesn’t feel the same. And the reason I like cash is that it works when the internet doesn’t.

Ana: OK, I see what you mean. So maybe we need both.

Leo: Exactly. That’s what I think too.

Choose the correct answer.

  • Who thinks cash will disappear soon?
  • What do Ana and Leo agree on at the end?

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the discussion between Ana and Leo. Answer the two questions.

Then two students read the dialogue aloud.

Say: Ana and Leo use some very useful phrases. Let’s find them.

5

Sound polite

6 min

Listen to your teacher. Then say these phrases politely.

  1. I’m not so sure.
  2. That’s a good point.
  3. I see what you mean, but...
  4. Sorry to interrupt, but...
  5. Maybe, but...

Teacher notes. 6 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say each phrase twice: once flat and rude, once with a friendly rise and fall. Students say which one is polite.

Drill chorally, then in pairs. Focus on a soft voice and a small pause before but.

Tip: smiling while you speak changes your intonation. Ask students to try it.

Pairs read it twice, swapping roles.

7

Speaking: the town meeting

13 min

The question: Should shops and cafés in your town be allowed to refuse cash?

Café owner

You want to go cashless. Cash takes time to count and you are worried about theft.

Grandparent

You don’t use cards. You don’t trust apps, and you are afraid of scams.

Bank manager

Cards are safer and faster. But your bank is closing branches in rural areas.

Student

You only use your phone. But last month the internet stopped and you couldn’t pay for lunch.

Steps

  1. Choose a role. Prepare three arguments.
  2. Hold the meeting. Everyone gives their opinion first.
  3. Discuss. Use phrases from the lesson. Interrupt politely at least once.
  4. Agree on a decision for the town and explain it to the class.

Opinions and reasons

  • In my opinion, ...
  • The reason is that...
  • For example, ...

Agreeing and disagreeing

  • That’s a good point.
  • I see what you mean, but...
  • I’m not so sure.

Interrupting

  • Sorry to interrupt, but...
  • Can I just say something?

Teacher notes. 13 min. Groups of four.

Say: There’s a town meeting today. Choose a role card and prepare three arguments. You have three minutes.

Groups of four, one role each. In threes, drop the bank manager. 3 min preparation, 7 min meeting, 3 min reporting.

Tick a checklist of phrases you hear for each student. Show it to them in feedback.

One-to-one: the student plays two roles in turn. You play the others.

8

Reflection and feedback

4 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 4 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘I am not agree.’ > ‘I don’t agree.’
  • ‘For my opinion’ > ‘In my opinion’
  • ‘pay with cash money’ > ‘pay in cash’
