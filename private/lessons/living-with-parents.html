Teacher's overview

Main aim: Speaking: to negotiate and reach agreement in a role-play.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for gist and detail. Vocabulary for home and money.

Background: According to Eurostat, young people in the EU leave home at around 26 on average. In Croatia, Spain and Italy the average is over 30, partly because of high housing costs.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. At what age do young people usually leave home in your country?
  2. What are the good and bad sides of living with your parents as an adult?
  3. Who does most of the housework in your home?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Let’s talk about family and home. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Give students 1 minute to think, then pairs discuss. Take two or three ideas from the class. Do not correct language here. This stage is about interest and ideas.

This topic can be cultural. In Armenia and Russia, living with family is often normal. Accept all views and keep the tone light.

Say: Great ideas. Today’s text has some useful words for this topic. Let’s look at them first.

3

First read

5 min

Read the text quickly. Choose the best summary.

Back in My Old Bedroom

More adults in their twenties live with their parents. For many, it’s a smart choice.

Daniel is 27. He has a good job as an engineer, but every evening he goes home to his parents’ flat. ‘I can’t afford to pay rent and save at the same time,’ he says. ‘Here I can save up for my own place.’

Daniel is not alone. Across Europe, the average young person leaves home at around 26. In Italy, Spain and Croatia, many stay until they are over 30. In Armenia, many young adults live with their family until they marry. Housing costs have risen fast, and many young people can’t find a flat they can pay for.

In some countries, there was once a stigma about living with your parents as an adult. People thought you were lazy or not independent. That idea is changing. In many cultures, families have always lived together, and young people don’t feel embarrassed about it.

Of course, sharing a home is not always easy. Adult children want privacy, and parents sometimes forget that their ‘baby’ is 27. Arguments about chores are common. ‘My mum keeps asking me to tidy my room,’ laughs Sofia, 25. ‘I’m an adult! But she’s right. I hate cleaning.’

Experts say the key is to treat the home like a shared household of adults. Agree on rules. Share the costs if you can. And talk openly. ‘I enjoy living with my parents now,’ says Daniel. ‘We cook together. I plan to move out next year, but I’ll miss this.’

Teacher notes. 5 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Don’t stop at new words. Choose the best summary. You have 3 minutes.

ICQs: “Do you need to understand every word?” (No.) “How many answers do you choose?” (One.)

Set a strict time limit. Pairs compare. In feedback ask: “Which part of the text helped you?”

Answer: B. Money is the main reason. The last paragraph gives advice for making it work.

Say: Now read again, more carefully this time.

4

Close read

8 min

Read again. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • Daniel doesn’t have a job.
  • In Spain, many young people stay at home until after 30.
  • People’s ideas about living with parents are changing.
  • Sofia enjoys cleaning.
  • Experts say families should agree on rules.
  • Daniel wants to live with his parents forever.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read again carefully. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)? Underline the part of the text that gives you the answer.

ICQ: “Do you need to show where the answer is?” (Yes.)

Pairs compare and show each other the evidence. In feedback, always ask “Where does it say that?”

Say: Well done. Now let’s look closely at some language from the text.

5

Language focus

10 min

Look at these sentences from the text.

  • I can’t afford to pay rent.
  • My mum keeps asking me to tidy my room.
  • I hate cleaning.
  • I enjoy living with my parents now.
  • I plan to move out next year.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Which verbs are followed by the -ing form?keep, hate, enjoy
  • Which verbs are followed by to + infinitive?afford, plan
  • Add one more verb to each group.Possible answers: -ing: avoid, finish, mind. to: decide, want, hope.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Focus: verb + -ing and verb + to + infinitive.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Work with a partner and answer the questions. Don’t worry about rules yet. Just look for patterns.

Monitor. Then elicit answers and build the rule on the board with the students. Students tell you the rule. You write it.

Rule for the board: Verb + -ing: enjoy, keep, avoid, finish, mind. Verb + to + infinitive: want, plan, decide, afford, hope, need. like, love and hate can take both with almost the same meaning.

CCQs: ‘I keep forgetting my keys.’ Did it happen once or many times? (Many times.) Am I happy about it? (No.)

Common mistake: ‘I enjoy to cook.’ enjoy is always followed by -ing.

Say: Now let’s practise it.

6

Practice

7 min

Complete the sentences with the -ing form or to + infinitive of the verb in brackets.

  • I’ve decided to move (move) out next year.
  • Do you mind doing (do) the washing-up tonight?
  • Many young people can’t afford to buy (buy) a flat.
  • My dad keeps telling (tell) me to turn off the lights.
  • I hope to find (find) a cheaper flat soon.
  • We try to avoid arguing (argue) about money.

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Work alone first. Complete the sentences. Then check with your partner.

ICQs: ‘Alone or with a partner first?’ (Alone.) ‘After enjoy, do we use -ing or to?’ (-ing.)

Fast finishers write one more sentence of their own with the target language.

In feedback, write the answers on the board. Focus on form errors. Ask “Why this form?” for one or two items.

Say: Perfect. Now you’re ready to use all of this in a real conversation.

7

Speaking

12 min

House rules

An adult child lives with their parent. They need new house rules. Negotiate and agree on five rules.

The parent

You want your adult child to help more at home. You want to know when they come home late. You don’t want to pay for everything.

The adult child

You want more privacy and freedom. You work long hours and you are tired in the evenings. You are saving up for a flat.

Topics to agree on

Chores. Money and bills. Guests. Noise at night. Meals.

Steps

  1. Choose a role and read your card.
  2. Think: what do you want? What can you give?
  3. Negotiate five house rules together.
  4. Write your rules. Read them to the class.

Useful language

I don’t mind doing... if you...I’d like you to...How about we...?That’s fair.I can’t promise to..., but...Let’s agree to...

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs or small groups.

Say: An adult child lives with their parent. You need new house rules. Choose a role and read your card. You have two minutes to prepare.

Pairs, one role each. 2 min preparation, 7 min negotiation, 3 min: pairs read their rules. The class votes for the fairest rule.

Give clear preparation time before students speak. Monitor closely. Write down good language and errors in two columns. Do not interrupt. Fluency comes first here.

One-to-one: you take the other role or position. Keep your turns short and push the student to justify and expand (“Why?”, “Can you give an example?”).

Say: Time’s up. Let’s look at some of the language you used.

8

Reflection and feedback

3 min

Think about today's lesson.

Teacher notes. 3 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board: some correct, some with errors. Do not say who said them.

Say: Here are some sentences I heard. Some are perfect. Some have a mistake. Work with your partner and find the mistakes.

Students correct in pairs. Elicit the corrections. Praise good language and highlight two or three great phrases students used.

Errors students often make with this lesson:

  • “I enjoy to live with my parents.” > “I enjoy living with my parents.”
  • “I can’t afford rent a flat.” > “I can’t afford to rent a flat.”
  • “My parents don’t let me to come home late.” > “My parents don’t let me come home late.”

Then students complete the reflection boxes. Ask one or two students to read theirs aloud.

Homework

Set at the end

A. Vocabulary review. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson. Change the form if you need to.

B. Writing: a forum post (110-140 words)

Reply to this forum question: ‘Is it OK to live with your parents at 28?’ Give your opinion, two reasons and describe the situation in your country.

Checklist

  • Use at least four verbs from the language focus, for example enjoy, plan, afford.
  • Use four words from the lesson.
  • Write in a friendly, polite tone.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and Part B. In Part B, use at least four words from today and the grammar we studied.

Collect the writing next lesson. Mark it for four things: task completion, vocabulary from the lesson, accuracy with the target language, and organisation.

Reward students who connect the topic to their own country with clear examples.

Start the next lesson with a two-minute vocabulary quiz using the words from Part A.
