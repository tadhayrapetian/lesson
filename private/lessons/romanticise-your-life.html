Make Ordinary Days Beautiful

Romanticise your life: write a descriptive blog post about small, happy moments.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • describe places and moments using the five senses
  • organise a blog post into clear paragraphs with sequence linkers
  • plan, write and check a descriptive blog post

Lesson 33

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Writing. Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Writing: a descriptive blog post, planned, drafted and peer-reviewed.

Subsidiary aims: Reading a model text for content and structure.

Language aim: Sense verbs (looks, smells, sounds like) and sequence linkers (first, while, as soon as, after that).

Background: ‘Romanticising your life’ became a popular social media idea: treating ordinary routines with care and attention, as if they were scenes from a film.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. What is a small thing that makes you happy every day?
  2. Have you heard of ‘romanticising your life’? What do you think it means?
  3. Do you take photos of ordinary moments, like your coffee or the sky?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Today you’re going to write a blog post about a happy, ordinary moment. First, discuss the questions.

Explain ‘romanticise your life’ with an example if needed: making your morning tea feel like a special ritual.

2

Read the model

8 min

My Perfect Tuesday Morning

1Last Tuesday was a normal day, but I decided to treat it like a scene from a film.

2First, I woke up early and opened the window. The air smelled fresh, like rain, and the street was quiet. While the kettle was boiling, I put on my favourite old song.

3Then I walked to a small café near my flat. Inside, it was warm and cosy. The coffee tasted rich and a little sweet, and the croissant was still warm. I sat by the window and watched people hurrying to work.

4After that, I took the long way to the office, through the park. The leaves were bright orange, and they made a soft sound under my feet.

5Nothing special happened, but I felt calm and happy all day. Romanticising your life isn’t about expensive things. It’s about noticing. Tomorrow, try it. Look up, slow down and enjoy one small moment.

Answer the questions.

  • Was Tuesday a special day?No. It was a normal day.
  • Which places did the writer go to?Home, a café and the park on the way to the office.
  • What is the writer’s main message?Notice and enjoy small moments. It isn’t about expensive things.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read this blog post. It’s our model. Answer the three questions.

Ask: ‘Did you like it? Which moment can you see in your head?’

3

How is it organised?

7 min

Match each paragraph to its purpose.

  • Paragraph 1e
  • Paragraph 2c
  • Paragraph 3b
  • Paragraph 4a
  • Paragraph 5d
  • aThe third moment, outside
  • bThe second moment, in a café
  • cThe first moment, at home
  • dConclusion and advice for the reader
  • eIntroduction: what kind of day it was

Teacher notes. 7 min. Pairs.

Say: A good blog post has a clear plan. What does each paragraph do? Match them.

Point out: short introduction, three moments, a conclusion that speaks to the reader. Students will copy this structure.

4

Language: the five senses

10 min

Choose the correct option.

  • The soup delicious.
  • It rain on the roof.
  • This scarf soft.
  • The cake chocolate.
  • You happy today.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Good descriptions use all five senses. Find which sense each phrase describes.

Before the second task, elicit the pattern: sense verb + adjective (smells delicious), sense verb + like + noun (smells like rain).

Common error: ‘It smells like delicious.’ Board the two patterns side by side.

5

Language: linking moments

7 min

Complete the story with the words in the box. Use each one once.

WhileFirstAfter thatFinallyAs soon as
  • First, I made a cup of tea.
  • While the tea was cooling, I opened my journal.
  • As soon as I opened the door, I smelled fresh bread.
  • I bought two warm rolls. After that, I sat in the park and ate them.
  • Finally, I went home and wrote about my morning.

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: These words show the order of events. Complete the mini story.

Highlight: while + an action in progress. as soon as = immediately when.

6

Plan

6 min

Teacher notes. 6 min. Individual.

Say: Choose a real day: yesterday, last weekend or a day you remember well. Make notes for each box. Notes, not full sentences.

Monitor and help with vocabulary. Push for sense details.

7

Write

12 min

Write your blog post (130-160 words).

Write a blog post about an ordinary day that you ‘romanticised’. Follow the model and your plan.

Checklist

  • Five short paragraphs: introduction, three moments, conclusion.
  • At least four senses and two sense verbs (looks, smells, sounds like).
  • At least three linkers: first, while, as soon as, after that, finally.
  • End with advice for your reader.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Individual.

Say: Now write your post. You have twelve minutes. Use your plan and the checklist.

Quiet writing time. Monitor and point to errors without correcting them for the student.

8

Peer review

5 min

Swap posts with your partner. Read and answer.

  1. Can you see the moments in your head? Which description do you like most?
  2. Find two linkers and three sense words.
  3. Give one suggestion to make the post better.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Pairs.

Say: Swap with your partner. Read their post and answer the three questions. Be kind and specific.

Model useful feedback language: ‘I love the part where...’, ‘Maybe you could add...’.

9

Homework

Set at the end

Write your final draft (130-160 words).

Rewrite your blog post using your partner’s suggestions. Add a title and describe the photo you would put at the top.

Checklist

  • Check spelling and past tense verbs.
  • Check the five-paragraph structure.
  • Add one new sense detail.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, write your final draft. Use your partner’s ideas.

Mark the final draft for content, organisation, vocabulary and accuracy. Give one ‘keep doing’ and one ‘try next time’ comment.
