Taking a Year Off

More people take career breaks, sometimes called ‘micro-retirements’. Learn to write a formal email asking for one.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • organise a formal email of request into clear paragraphs
  • change informal language into a formal, polite register
  • plan, write and peer-review a persuasive formal email

Background: ‘Micro-retirement’ became a popular term in 2025 for planned career breaks of a few months to a year. Many employers offer unpaid sabbaticals to keep experienced staff.

1

Warm-up

6 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. If you could take six months off work or study, what would you do?
  2. Have you heard of a ‘gap year’ or a ‘sabbatical’?
  3. Is it risky to take a career break? Why?

Teacher notes. 6 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Imagine six months with no work or study. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Say: Today you’ll write a formal email to ask for a career break.

2

Context

8 min

Retire Now, Work Later?

Traditionally, people worked for forty years and then retired. But a growing number of younger workers are choosing a different path: ‘micro-retirements’. These are planned breaks of a few months to a year, taken between jobs or agreed with an employer as a sabbatical. People use them to travel, care for family, learn a skill or simply recover from stress.

Supporters say a break can prevent burnout and make people more creative when they return. Critics point out that not everyone can afford months without a salary, and that a long gap on a CV can worry some employers. Even so, many companies now offer unpaid sabbaticals to keep experienced staff.

Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • Micro-retirements usually last for many years.
  • Some people agree a break with their employer.
  • Everyone can afford a micro-retirement.
  • Some companies offer sabbaticals to keep staff.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the short article. True or false?

Ask: ‘Would your employer or university agree to this?’

3

Read the model

8 min

Subject: Request for a six-month sabbatical

Dear Ms Novak,

1I am writing to request a six-month unpaid sabbatical, starting on 1 March next year.

2I have worked in the marketing team for five years, and during this time I have led several successful campaigns. I would now like to take some time to complete a course in data analysis and to care for my father, who is recovering from an operation.

3I believe the break would also benefit the company. The skills I gain would allow me to support the team’s new digital strategy when I return. Before I leave, I would be happy to train a colleague to cover my main responsibilities.

4I would be grateful if we could arrange a meeting to discuss this request. Would it be possible to meet next week?

5Thank you for considering my request. I look forward to hearing from you. Kind regards, Daniel Hakobyan

Answer the questions.

  • What is the main purpose of the email?
  • How does Daniel try to persuade his manager?

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read Daniel’s email. It’s our model. Answer the questions.

Ask: ‘Would you say yes if you were Ms Novak? Why?’

4

How is it organised?

7 min

Match each paragraph to its purpose.

  • Paragraph 1e
  • Paragraph 2c
  • Paragraph 3d
  • Paragraph 4b
  • Paragraph 5a
  • aA polite closing
  • bThe next step
  • cBackground and personal reasons
  • dBenefits for the company and a practical solution
  • eThe reason for writing

Teacher notes. 7 min. Pairs.

Say: What does each paragraph do? Match them.

Highlight: the purpose comes in the very first sentence. Busy managers need to know immediately.

5

Formal language

13 min

Match the informal phrases 1-7 to the formal phrases a-g.

  • Hi Anna,d
  • I want to ask for...f
  • Can we meet?a
  • I’d love it if...c
  • I’ll get someone to cover for me.g
  • Thanks for thinking about it.b
  • Write back soon!e
  • aWould it be possible to arrange a meeting?
  • bThank you for considering my request.
  • cI would be grateful if...
  • dDear Ms Novak,
  • eI look forward to hearing from you.
  • fI am writing to request...
  • gI would be happy to train a colleague to cover my responsibilities.

Formal emails usually avoid contractions (I’m > I am), many phrasal verbs and exclamation marks.

Make these sentences more formal.

  • I’m writing because I wanna take some time off.I am writing to request some time off.
  • Can you get back to me ASAP?I would be grateful if you could reply at your earliest convenience.
  • I’ll sort out all my work before I go.I will complete all my work before I leave.
  • Let me know what you think!I look forward to hearing your thoughts.

Teacher notes. 13 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: First, match the informal and formal phrases. Then rewrite the four sentences in a formal style.

Accept different correct formal versions in the second task.

Common problem: students mix registers, for example ‘Dear Ms Novak’ and then ‘Thanks a lot!!’. Point this out.

6

Plan

8 min

Situation A

You want three months off to volunteer on an environmental project abroad.

Situation B

You want six months off to write a book.

Situation C

You want to work part-time for a year to care for a family member.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual.

Say: Choose a situation, or use your own real situation. Make notes for each paragraph.

Monitor. Push students to think from the manager’s point of view: ‘What will worry your manager?’

7

Write

18 min

Write your formal email (180-220 words).

Write your email requesting a career break. Follow the model structure and your plan.

Checklist

  • The purpose is clear in the first sentence.
  • Five paragraphs, each with one purpose.
  • Formal language throughout: no contractions or slang.
  • At least one benefit for the organisation and one practical solution.

Teacher notes. 18 min. Individual.

Say: Now write your email. You have eighteen minutes. Use your plan and the checklist.

Quiet writing time. Point to errors with a question rather than correcting them.

8

Peer review

7 min

Swap emails with your partner. Check:

  1. Is the purpose clear in the first paragraph?
  2. Is the tone formal throughout? Underline anything too informal.
  3. Does the writer explain a benefit for the organisation?
  4. Is there a clear next step?

Teacher notes. 7 min. Pairs.

Say: Swap emails. Read your partner’s email like a manager. Answer the four questions.

Model kind, specific feedback: ‘Your request is clear, but paragraph 3 could...’

9

Homework

Set at the end

Write a reply from the manager (150-180 words).

Imagine you are the manager who receives your partner’s email. Write a formal reply. Either accept the request with conditions, or politely refuse and offer an alternative.

Checklist

  • Thank the writer and refer to their request.
  • Give clear reasons for your decision.
  • Use formal language and a polite closing.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, write the manager’s reply. Then correct your own email using your partner’s feedback.

Mark both texts for task, organisation, register and accuracy.
