Fur Babies

More people call their pets family. Are we spoiling them?

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • talk about experiences and situations up to now
  • choose between the present perfect simple and continuous
  • ask and answer How long...? questions with for and since

Background: Spending on pets has grown fast in many countries, and ‘pet parent’ is now a common phrase. Vets in several countries report more overweight pets.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. Do you have a pet now, or did you have one as a child?
  2. Is a pet a member of the family? Why or why not?
  3. What is the strangest thing you have seen someone buy for a pet?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Let’s talk about animals. Discuss the questions with your partner.

If students don’t have pets, they can talk about friends’ or neighbours’ pets.

Say: Now read about a couple who call their dog their baby.

2

Read

9 min

Meet the Pet Parents

Ani and Davit have been married for six years. They don’t have children, but they have had a dog, a fluffy white Samoyed called Snow, since 2021. ‘Snow is our baby,’ Ani laughs. ‘We’ve celebrated every one of her birthdays with a special dog cake.’

Ani and Davit are part of a big change. Many young adults are having fewer children, and many of them treat their pets like family. Pet shops now sell raincoats, strollers and even vitamins for cats. Some offices allow dogs at work.

Not everyone is happy about it. Vets say they have been seeing more overweight pets, because owners give them too many treats. ‘A dog needs walks, not cupcakes,’ says one vet.

Ani agrees, partly. ‘We’ve been running with Snow every morning since spring. She’s never been so fit. But she still gets her birthday cake!’

Choose the correct answer.

  • How long has Ani had Snow?
  • What problem do vets see?
  • What has Ani been doing every morning since spring?

Teacher notes. 9 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text and answer the three questions. You have four minutes.

Pairs compare, then quick feedback.

Say: Good. Now let’s look at some of the verbs in the text.

3

Language focus

12 min

Look at these examples.

  • Ani and Davit have been married for six years.
  • ...they have had a dog since 2021.
  • We’ve celebrated every one of her birthdays with a special dog cake.
  • Vets say they have been seeing more overweight pets.
  • We’ve been running with Snow every morning since spring.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Which verbs use have / has + past participle? Which use have / has been + -ing?Simple: have been married, have had, have celebrated. Continuous: have been seeing, have been running.
  • When do we use for, and when since?for + a period of time (six years). since + a starting point (2021, spring).
  • Why do we say ‘have had a dog’, not ‘have been having a dog’?have (meaning own) is a state verb. State verbs (have, be, know, like) don’t usually take the continuous form.

Rule for the board: present perfect simple and continuous

Present perfect simple (have / has + past participle): states up to now (I’ve had a dog for years), results and how many times (We’ve celebrated every birthday). Present perfect continuous (have / has been + -ing): activities that started in the past and continue now, with a focus on duration (We’ve been running since spring). for + period, since + point in time. State verbs (have = own, be, know, like, want) use the simple form.

CCQs: ‘I’ve been walking the dog.’ Is the activity recent? (Yes.) ‘I’ve walked the dog three times today.’ Do we know how many times? (Yes.)

Russian and Armenian speakers often use the present simple: ‘I have this cat for five years.’ Contrast it with ‘I’ve had this cat for five years.’ Also ‘since five years’ > ‘for five years’.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Underline the verbs. Then answer the questions with your partner.

Draw a timeline on the board from 2021 to now for ‘have had a dog’.

Say: Now let’s practise.

5

Practice: simple or continuous?

7 min

Choose the correct form.

  • We our cat for twelve years.
  • My neighbour’s dog all morning. I can’t work!
  • I the dog three times today.
  • She her vet since she was a child.
  • You look tired. with the puppy?
  • They a new bed for the dog. It arrived yesterday.

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Choose the correct form. Think: is it a state? Do we count how many times? Is the activity still going on?

ICQ: ‘Can know be continuous?’ (No. It’s a state verb.)

In feedback, ask students to explain items 2 and 3.

6

Practice: ask your partner

8 min

Complete the questions. Then ask and answer with a partner.

  • How long have you lived / have you been living (you / live) in this city?
  • How long have you known (you / know) your best friend?
  • How long have you been learning (you / learn) English?
  • How many countries have you visited (you / visit)?
  • What have you been doing (you / do) in your free time recently?

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: First, complete the questions alone. Then ask your partner. Give long answers.

Item 1 has two correct answers. Ask students why. (live works in both forms.)

Students report one interesting answer to the class.

7

Speaking: the adoption interview

10 min

An animal shelter has dogs, cats, rabbits and a parrot. One student works at the shelter. The other wants to adopt an animal.

Shelter worker

You want to find the right home for each animal. Ask about the person’s home, free time and experience with animals.

Adopter A

You’ve lived in a small flat for two years. You’ve been working from home since January. You’ve never had a pet.

Adopter B

You’ve had cats all your life. You’ve been looking for a new cat for months. You have two small children.

Steps

  1. Choose your roles.
  2. The shelter worker asks at least six questions with How long...? and Have you ever...?
  3. The shelter worker decides which animal is right for this person and explains why.
  4. Swap roles. Use the other adopter card.

Useful language

How long have you lived...?Have you ever had...?What have you been doing to prepare?I’ve been thinking about it for...I’ve never..., but...I think a... would be perfect for you.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs.

Say: One of you works at an animal shelter. The other wants to adopt. Read your card. You have one minute to prepare.

Pairs, 4 min per interview, then swap. Monitor for for / since and state verbs.

One-to-one: be the shelter worker first so the student hears the model questions. Then swap.

8

Reflection and feedback

4 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 4 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘I have this dog since 2020.’ > ‘I’ve had this dog since 2020.’
  • ‘since five years’ > ‘for five years’
  • ‘She has been knowing him’ > ‘She has known him’
9

Homework

Set at the end

A. Each sentence has one mistake. Write the correct sentence.

  • I have this dog since 2020.I have had this dog since 2020.
  • She has been knowing him for years.She has known him for years.
  • We’ve been living here since five years.We’ve been living here for five years. / We’ve lived here for five years.
  • How long do you have your cat?How long have you had your cat?
  • I’ve walked in the park all morning, so I’m tired.I’ve been walking in the park all morning, so I’m tired.

Write an email to a friend (80-100 words).

Write about a pet you have, had, or would like. Say how long you’ve had it (or wanted it), what you’ve been doing together recently, and why it’s special.

Checklist

  • Use the present perfect simple and continuous.
  • Use for and since correctly.
  • Start and finish like a real email.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, correct the five sentences and write the email.

Next lesson, students read their emails in pairs and find one present perfect continuous verb in their partner’s text.
