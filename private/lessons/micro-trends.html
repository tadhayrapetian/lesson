Trends That Last Five Minutes

Social media is changing how fast fashion moves, and how young people shop

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • understand an article about fashion and social media
  • choose between the past simple and the present perfect
  • discuss shopping habits and give advice

Teacher's overview

Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Speaking: to rank shopping habits and agree on advice.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for gist and detail. Vocabulary for fashion and social media.

Language aim: Present perfect and past simple.

Background: Micro-trends are short fashion trends that spread on social media, especially TikTok. Named looks such as ‘clean girl’ or ‘mob wife’ were popular for short periods in 2023 and 2024.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. What clothes are popular with young people in your city right now?
  2. Have you ever bought something because you saw it online? What was it?
  3. How long do you usually keep your clothes?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Fashion changes very quickly today. Let’s talk about it. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Give students 1 minute to think, then pairs discuss. Take two or three ideas from the class. Do not correct language here. This stage is about interest and ideas.

Say: Great ideas. Today’s text has some useful words for this topic. Let’s look at them first.

3

First read

5 min

Read the text quickly. Choose the best summary.

Here Today, Gone Tomorrow

Fashion trends once lasted for years. Now some disappear in weeks. Why?

Have you heard of ‘mob wife’, ‘clean girl’ or ‘coastal grandmother’? These are all fashion aesthetics that went viral on TikTok in the last few years. Each one was everywhere for a few weeks. Then, suddenly, people forgot about it.

Experts call these ‘micro-trends’. In the past, a fashion trend often lasted for five years or more. Today, social media moves much faster. A video of one outfit can reach millions of people in a day, and online shops copy the look in about a week.

Fast fashion shops have made these clothes very affordable. A top can cost less than a coffee. But cheap clothes come with a cost. Many people wear an item only a few times. Then it goes in the bin. Clothing waste has grown a lot since the 2000s.

Influencers play a big part. Their videos influence what millions of teenagers want to buy. ‘I bought a pair of trousers because everyone on my feed had them,’ says Zoe, 17. ‘Two months later, nobody wore them. I’ve only worn them twice.’

But some young people are pushing back. They buy second-hand clothes, swap with friends or build a small wardrobe of pieces they really love. ‘I’ve stopped following trends,’ says Ali, 20. ‘I found my own style last year, and I’ve saved a lot of money.’

Teacher notes. 5 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Don’t stop at new words. Choose the best summary. You have 3 minutes.

ICQs: “Do you need to understand every word?” (No.) “How many answers do you choose?” (One.)

Set a strict time limit. Pairs compare. In feedback ask: “Which part of the text helped you?”

Answer: A. The text explains the speed of trends and ends with how some young people react.

Say: Now read again, more carefully this time.

4

Close read

8 min

Read again. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • The ‘clean girl’ look stayed popular for many years.
  • Online shops can copy a viral look in about a week.
  • The text says fast fashion clothes are expensive.
  • Zoe has worn her trousers many times.
  • Ali has saved money.
  • Some young people exchange clothes with friends.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read again carefully. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)? Underline the part of the text that gives you the answer.

ICQ: “Do you need to show where the answer is?” (Yes.)

Pairs compare and show each other the evidence. In feedback, always ask “Where does it say that?”

Say: Well done. Now let’s look closely at some language from the text.

5

Language focus

10 min

Look at these sentences from the text.

  • Each one was everywhere for a few weeks.
  • I’ve only worn them twice.
  • I found my own style last year.
  • Clothing waste has grown a lot since the 2000s.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Which sentences talk about a finished time in the past?‘was everywhere’ and ‘found my own style last year’.
  • Which sentences connect the past with now?‘I’ve only worn them twice’ and ‘has grown since the 2000s’.
  • How do we make the present perfect?have / has + past participle: worn, grown.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Focus: present perfect and past simple.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Work with a partner and answer the questions. Don’t worry about rules yet. Just look for patterns.

Monitor. Then elicit answers and build the rule on the board with the students. Students tell you the rule. You write it.

Rule for the board: Past simple: a finished action at a finished time (yesterday, last year, in 2020). Present perfect = have / has + past participle: experience or change up to now, with no finished time (ever, never, just, already, since, so far).

CCQs: ‘I’ve bought three jackets this year.’ Is this year finished? (No.) ‘I bought a jacket last year.’ Is last year finished? (Yes.)

Students often say ‘I have bought it yesterday.’ With a finished time word, use the past simple.

Say: Now let’s practise it.

6

Practice

7 min

Complete the sentences with the past simple or the present perfect of the verb in brackets.

  • I bought (buy) this jacket last winter.
  • Have you ever sold (sell) clothes online?
  • She hasn’t followed (not follow) any trends since she started university.
  • The video got (get) two million views yesterday.
  • My sister has already given (already / give) her old clothes to a charity shop.
  • I have never tried (never / try) a clothes swap. Is it fun?

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Work alone first. Complete the sentences. Then check with your partner.

ICQs: ‘Alone or with a partner first?’ (Alone.) ‘If you see yesterday or last month, which tense do you use?’ (Past simple.)

Fast finishers write one more sentence of their own with the target language.

In feedback, write the answers on the board. Focus on form errors. Ask “Why this form?” for one or two items.

Say: Perfect. Now you’re ready to use all of this in a real conversation.

7

Speaking

12 min

Trend or treasure?

Here are six ways to shop. Which are good for your wallet? Which are good for the planet?

The influencer buy

Buying a top because an influencer wore it.

The vintage hunter

Buying second-hand clothes.

The renter

Renting clothes for a special event.

The swapper

Swapping clothes with friends.

The investor

Buying one expensive item that lasts ten years.

The sale shopper

Buying clothes only in sales.

Steps

  1. Alone, rank the six habits from best to worst for your wallet.
  2. Now rank them for the planet. Compare both lists with your partner.
  3. Tell your partner about the last thing you bought. Was it a trend?
  4. Agree on three tips for a teenager who wants to shop smarter.

Useful language

I’ve never tried...The last thing I bought was...It’s affordable, but...It’s better for the planet because...I’d put this first because...I’m not so sure about that.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs or small groups.

Say: Look at the six ways to shop. First rank them alone. Then compare with your partner. You have two minutes to rank.

Pairs. 2 min ranking alone, 6 min comparing, 3 min agreeing on tips. Collect the best tips on the board.

Give clear preparation time before students speak. Monitor closely. Write down good language and errors in two columns. Do not interrupt. Fluency comes first here.

One-to-one: you take the other role or position. Keep your turns short and push the student to justify and expand (“Why?”, “Can you give an example?”).

Say: Time’s up. Let’s look at some of the language you used.

8

Reflection and feedback

3 min

Think about today's lesson.

Teacher notes. 3 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board: some correct, some with errors. Do not say who said them.

Say: Here are some sentences I heard. Some are perfect. Some have a mistake. Work with your partner and find the mistakes.

Students correct in pairs. Elicit the corrections. Praise good language and highlight two or three great phrases students used.

Errors students often make with this lesson:

  • “I have bought this dress last week.” > “I bought this dress last week.”
  • “This outfit is very popular in this days.” > “This outfit is very popular these days.”
  • “Clothes from second hand are cheaper.” > “Second-hand clothes are cheaper.”

Then students complete the reflection boxes. Ask one or two students to read theirs aloud.

9

Homework

Set at the end

A. Vocabulary review. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson. Change the form if you need to.

wardrobeoutfitwent viralinfluencesecond-hand
  • I wore my favourite outfit to the party: jeans, a white shirt and a black jacket.
  • The video of the dancing cat went viral and got ten million views.
  • This shop sells second-hand clothes, so you can find good brands at low prices.
  • My wardrobe is full, but I always feel I have nothing to wear.
  • Music videos can influence how young people dress.

B. Writing: an opinion paragraph (100-130 words)

Answer this question: ‘Should teenagers follow fashion trends?’ Give your opinion, two reasons and one example from your life or from someone you know.

Checklist

  • Use the present perfect and the past simple correctly.
  • Use four words from the lesson.
  • Finish with a clear conclusion.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and Part B. In Part B, use at least four words from today and the grammar we studied.

Collect the writing next lesson. Mark it for four things: task completion, vocabulary from the lesson, accuracy with the target language, and organisation.

Check that students use the past simple with finished time words in their example.

Start the next lesson with a two-minute vocabulary quiz using the words from Part A.
