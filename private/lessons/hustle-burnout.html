Hustle Culture & Burnout

Somewhere between "rise and grind" and running on empty, an entire generation redefined exhaustion as ambition. Let's talk about what we actually owe our jobs — and what we don't.

Warm-Up

Lead-In Discussion

Talk in pairs or small groups for 5–6 minutes before we look at any vocabulary.

  • 1.How much of your sense of self-worth is tied to how productive or "busy" you appear to others — and is that healthy?
  • 2.Can genuine ambition and real rest coexist, or is one always sacrificed for the other? Give an example from your own life.
  • 3.Do you think older and younger generations at work genuinely disagree about overwork, or do they just talk about it differently?
  • 4.Is it possible to "quietly quit" — doing only what you're paid to do, nothing more — without it damaging your career or your conscience?

Grammar Focus

Modals of Advice & Obligation

Advice (should / ought to): used to say what is a good idea, based on opinion or experience. Not legally or contractually binding.

Obligation — external (must / have to): have to usually expresses a rule or requirement imposed by someone else (a boss, a law, a contract). Must can express the same external rule, but is also used for the speaker's own strong, internal sense of necessity or urgency.

Need to: expresses a practical necessity — something is genuinely required to reach a goal, often slightly softer and more personal than must.

Had better: stronger than should, and carries an implied warning — if the advice is ignored, there will be a negative consequence, often fairly soon.

Should have + past participle: used to criticize or express regret about a past action (or inaction) that cannot be changed now.

Comparing strength & nuance
Modal Strength Nuance / Use Example
should / ought to Mild Opinion-based advice; ought to is more formal, less common in speech You should delegate more instead of doing everything yourself.
need to Moderate Practical necessity to achieve a result You need to log off by six if you want to actually recover.
have to Strong External obligation — a rule, contract, or someone else's requirement All staff have to complete the wellbeing survey this month.
must Strong External rule OR the speaker's own urgent, internal conviction I must stop answering emails after 9pm — it's ruining my sleep.
had better Very strong Advice + implied warning of a bad consequence if ignored You'd better talk to HR before this affects your health.
should have + p.p. Retrospective Regret or criticism about the unchangeable past He shouldn't have taken on that second side hustle — he had nothing left to give.
  • 1. Managers ought to notice the early warning signs of burnout before an employee reaches breaking point.
  • 2. You don't have to answer messages on the weekend — that's not in your contract, no matter what the culture implies.
  • 3. If you keep burning the candle at both ends like this, you'd better expect your health to suffer sooner or later.
  • 4. I must admit — the "always on" mentality got the better of me long before I recognized it as a problem.
  • 5. She should have set boundaries with her clients years ago instead of quietly resenting every late-night call.
Controlled Practice

Exercises

Choose the best option or complete the sentence. Discuss your reasoning with a partner.

1.Your colleague looks exhausted and keeps skipping lunch. What's the most natural piece of advice?

2.Complete with the correct form of have to: "All new employees ______ (have to) attend the mandatory onboarding session next Monday."

3.Rewrite using should have + past participle: "It was a mistake to ignore the early signs of burnout for so long."

4.Which sentence best expresses an internal, personal sense of urgency rather than an external company rule?

5.Fill the gap with need to, must, or have to (more than one may be possible — be ready to explain your choice): "If we want the team to stop burning out, we ______ change how we measure 'productivity'."

6.Choose the sentence that carries a clear warning of consequence.

7.Transform into the negative obligation with the correct meaning: "It is not necessary for you to reply to emails after hours." → "You ______ reply to emails after hours."

8.Find and correct the error: "She should to have talked to a therapist months ago, before the exhaustion became unbearable."

9.Which modal best fits: "Honestly, nobody ______ work sixty-hour weeks to prove their worth — that idea itself is the problem."

10.Complete with mustn't or don't have to: "You ______ feel guilty for logging off on time — it's not a failure, it's a boundary."

Speaking Practice

Role-Play: "You Look Burned Out"

Work in pairs. Choose a card each and act out the conversation. Use at least three modals of advice/obligation from today's lesson.

Role A — The Colleague

You've noticed your coworker hasn't taken a real day off in three months. They cancel plans constantly and seem irritable. Start a caring but honest conversation. Give them concrete advice using should, had better, and need to.

Role B — The Overworked One

You insist you're "fine," but admit you feel overwhelmed and haven't unwound in weeks. Explain what you think you have to do for your job, and react to your colleague's advice — agree with some points, push back on others.

Role A (Round 2) — The Manager

One of your team members has quietly started doing the bare minimum. Talk to them about expectations and support, using must/have to for real requirements and should for genuine advice — avoid sounding punitive.

Role B (Round 2) — The Quiet Quitter

You're not lazy — you're protecting your energy after last year's burnout. Explain, using shouldn't have, what you regret about how you worked before, and what boundaries you now refuse to cross.

Discussion Grid: Workplace Dilemmas

In small groups, discuss each dilemma. For each one, agree on what the person should/shouldn't/must/needs to do.

A friend brags about only sleeping five hours a night and calls it "dedication." What should you say to them?
Your company offers "unlimited vacation," but no one on the team actually takes more than five days a year. What needs to change?
A junior employee keeps volunteering for extra unpaid projects to "stand out." What advice would you give them?
An employee quietly reduced their output after being denied a promotion. Was this the right response, or should they have done something different?
Production Task

Longer Speaking Turn (2–3 minutes)

Prepare for two minutes, then speak for 2–3 minutes without stopping. Your partner listens and notes down every modal verb they hear.

Topic: "What I think workplaces — and individuals — should actually change about how we work."

Use these guiding points, and try to naturally include at least five modals of advice/obligation and five vocabulary items from today:

  • Describe a time you (or someone you know) experienced burnout, overwork, or the pressure of hustle culture.
  • Explain what that person should have done differently, looking back.
  • Say what you believe employers must/have to change about workplace expectations.
  • Say what individuals need to do for themselves to protect their work-life balance.
  • End with one piece of advice you'd give your younger, more "hustle-obsessed" self.
