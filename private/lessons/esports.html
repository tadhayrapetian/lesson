Is Gaming a Sport?

Esports fill stadiums and pay millions. But are gamers really athletes?

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • understand the main ideas and details of a radio discussion
  • use phrases to agree, partly agree and disagree politely
  • take part in a short debate

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Listening. Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Listening: for gist and for specific information in a radio discussion.

Subsidiary aims: Speaking: a mini debate. Vocabulary for sport and competition.

Language aim: Functional language: agreeing, partly agreeing and disagreeing.

Background: Professional esports tournaments offer prize pools of millions of dollars and attract huge online audiences. Shooting and archery are Olympic sports. Chess is not.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. Do you play video games? Which ones?
  2. What makes something a sport? Think of three things.
  3. Have you ever watched other people play video games online?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Today’s question is: is gaming a sport? Discuss these questions with your partner.

Collect students’ ideas for ‘what makes a sport’ on the board. Keep them for the debate.

3

Predict

4 min

You will hear a radio show. A gaming coach and a former Olympic swimmer discuss the question: is esports a real sport?

Teacher notes. 4 min. Pairs.

Say: What will each person say? Write one or two ideas for each.

Prediction gives students a reason to listen. Take a few ideas, but don’t confirm them yet.

4

First listening

6 min

Sport Talk, radio show
Your teacher will play or read the recording. Listen carefully. You don’t need to understand every word.

Audio script: Sport Talk, radio show. Read it aloud at natural speed, or record it in advance with a colleague.

Host: Welcome back to Sport Talk. Today’s question: is esports a real sport? With me are Kevin Park, a coach for a professional gaming team, and Helen Brooks, a former Olympic swimmer. Kevin, let’s start with you.

Kevin: Thanks. People think gamers just sit and play all day. But my players train for eight to ten hours a day. They practise strategy, they watch videos of other teams, and they go to the gym every morning. Their reactions have to be incredibly fast. Some players make more than three hundred actions per minute.

Host: Three hundred actions a minute! Helen, is that enough to call it a sport?

Helen: It’s impressive, I agree. Those players are skilled, and they work hard. But for me, a sport needs physical effort. When I was swimming, I trained in the pool for five hours a day. My whole body was exhausted. A gamer uses their hands and their eyes, but they don’t sweat like a footballer.

Kevin: Well, what about chess? Or archery? Or shooting? They’re in the Olympics, and they don’t need much physical effort either.

Helen: That’s a fair point. Chess isn’t in the Olympics, actually, but shooting is. Hmm. OK, I’ll accept that the line isn’t always clear.

Host: And what about the money? Kevin, how big is esports now?

Kevin: It’s huge. The biggest tournaments have prizes of millions of dollars, and millions of people watch online. Some finals fill whole stadiums.

Host: Helen, any worries?

Helen: Yes, health. Young players often sit for too long, sleep badly and hurt their wrists. And many careers finish before players are twenty-five.

Kevin: That’s true, and good teams take it seriously now. We have a physiotherapist, and our players have to sleep eight hours and exercise every day.

Host: So, final answer: sport or not?

Helen: A competition, yes. A sport... I’m still not sure!

Kevin: It’s a sport. Come and watch a final, Helen. Then decide.

Listen. Choose the correct answer. Were your predictions right?

  • Who are the two guests?
  • At the end, Helen thinks esports is...

Teacher notes. 6 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Listen to the radio show. Answer the two questions. Then check your predictions.

Read the script once at natural speed. Use different voices for each speaker.

Pairs compare. Check predictions from stage 3.

5

Second listening

11 min

Listen again. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • Kevin’s players train for eight to ten hours a day.
  • Kevin’s players never do physical exercise.
  • Helen trained in the pool for three hours a day.
  • Chess is an Olympic sport.
  • Some esports finals take place in stadiums.
  • Kevin’s team has a physiotherapist.

Complete the notes with one or two words or a number.

  • Some players make more than three hundred / 300 actions per minute.
  • Young players can hurt their wrists.
  • Many careers finish before players are twenty-five / 25.
  • Kevin’s players have to sleep eight / 8 hours.

Teacher notes. 11 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Listen again. First do true or false. Then complete the notes. Read the questions before I start.

Read the script a second time. If students need it, read the key parts a third time.

In feedback, read the sentence that gives each answer.

6

Language from the show

7 min

Complete what the speakers said. Use one word in each gap.

  • It’s impressive, I agree.
  • That’s a fair point.
  • I’ll accept that the line isn’t always clear.
  • That’s true, and good teams take it seriously now.
  • I’m still not sure!

Agreeing

  • I agree.
  • That’s true.
  • Exactly.

Partly agreeing

  • That’s a fair point, but...
  • I’ll accept that..., but...
  • It’s impressive, but...

Disagreeing politely

  • I’m not sure about that.
  • I see what you mean, but...
  • For me, ...

Teacher notes. 7 min. Pairs.

Say: Can you remember the missing words? Complete the sentences with your partner. Then I’ll read those lines again.

Read only the lines with the target phrases.

Drill the phrases with polite intonation. ‘That’s a fair point, but...’ needs a small pause before but.

7

Speaking: mini debate

14 min

Debate: Should esports be in the Olympic Games?

Steps

  1. Decide your opinion. Write three reasons.
  2. In groups, each person gives their opinion in one minute.
  3. Discuss. Use the phrases for agreeing and disagreeing. Everyone must use at least three.
  4. Vote. Then tell the class your group’s decision.

Useful language

In my opinion, ...That’s a fair point, but...I’ll accept that..., but...For me, a sport needs...I’m not sure about that.Exactly!

Teacher notes. 14 min. Groups of three or four.

Say: Should esports be in the Olympics? Decide your opinion and write three reasons. You have three minutes.

Groups of three or four. 3 min preparation, 8 min debate, 3 min reporting.

One-to-one: take the opposite view to the student and push them to use the phrases.

8

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘I am agree.’ > ‘I agree.’
  • ‘It’s depend on the game.’ > ‘It depends on the game.’
  • ‘Gamers don’t do any sport.’ > ‘Gamers don’t do any exercise.’
