Topic: Friendship & Ghosting ⏱ ~60 min

Ghosted: Why Adult Friendships Fade — and How We Talk About It

People don't usually break up with their friends — they just stop replying. This lesson looks at how friendships quietly end, and gives you the grammar to report what was said when they did.

Warm-up

Before we start

Discuss these questions with a partner. There are no easy answers — that's the point.

  • 1.Is it ever kinder to simply disappear from a friendship than to have an honest, awkward conversation about why it's ending?
  • 2.Do you think adults need "closure" when a friendship ends, or is that just a way of avoiding moving on?
  • 3.Why do you think friendships get harder to maintain as people get older — is it really about having less time, or something else?
  • 4.Have you ever realised, only in hindsight, that a friendship had become one-sided? What was the first sign?

Grammar focus

Reported speech: statements & questions

When we report what someone said or asked, the sentence usually moves one step "back" — in tense, in time, and in perspective.

1. Reporting statements

We generally introduce the reported clause with said (that) or told + person (that). If the reporting verb is in the past, the verb tense in the reported clause usually shifts "one step back" (backshift):

  • present simple → past simple
  • present continuous → past continuous
  • present perfect → past perfect
  • past simple → past perfect (often, though not always required)
  • will → would  |  can → could  |  may → might  |  must → had to / must

Pronouns, possessives, and time/place expressions also shift to match the reporter's point of view: I→she/he, my→her/his, now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day, yesterday→the day before, here→there, this→that.

2. Reporting questions

Reported questions follow statement word order (subject + verb) — there is no auxiliary do/does/did, no inversion, and no question mark.

  • Yes/No questions: introduce with if or whether.
    "Did you get my message?" → He asked me if / whether I had got his message.
  • Wh- questions: keep the question word, but switch to normal word order.
    "Why didn't you reply?" → She asked me why I hadn't replied.
Direct speech vs. reported speech
Direct speechReported speech
“I'm too busy to hang out,” she said.She said (that) she was too busy to hang out.
“I haven't heard from you in months,” he told me.He told me (that) he hadn't heard from me in months.
“Why didn't you reply to my texts?” she asked me.She asked me why I hadn't replied to her texts.
“Did you get my message?” he asked.He asked me if/whether I had got his message.
“I'll call you back tomorrow,” she promised.She promised she would call me back the next day.
  • My old friend claimed she'd never received my messages, even though I knew she had read them.
  • He admitted that he'd been avoiding me because he didn't know how to explain his feelings.
  • She asked whether I'd noticed that our friendship had become one-sided.
  • They explained that they had simply grown apart after starting new jobs.
  • He denied that he had been ignoring me on purpose.

Speaking practice

Interview: a friendship that faded

Work in pairs. Interview your partner using the questions below (or your own follow-up questions). Take brief notes, then report their answers back to the class or another pair using reported speech — e.g. "She told me that she'd stopped calling because…" or "He said he still didn't know why they'd fallen out."

Question to ask your partnerNotes on their answer
Tell me about a friendship that ended or faded away. What happened?
Did the friendship end suddenly, or did you drift apart slowly?
Did you ever try to reach out again? What happened?
Looking back, is there anything you wish you'd said?
Do you think you got closure, or does it still feel unfinished?

Optional role-play — Role A

You haven't heard from a close friend in eight months. You finally message them and ask to meet. Confront them gently: ask why they disappeared, and how they'd explain the silence.

Optional role-play — Role B

You ghosted a friend without meaning to — life got overwhelming and you didn't know how to reply after so much time had passed. Explain yourself when they confront you.

After the role-play, report to another pair what was said: "He asked her why she'd stopped replying, and she explained that…"

Production task

Longer speaking turn (2–3 minutes)

Prepare a short talk about a friendship that ended, faded, or was ghosted — your own story or an invented one. Use the points below to structure it.

Include the following:

  • Describe the friendship and how it started to change — use at least three vocabulary items from this lesson (e.g. drift apart, one-sided, take for granted).
  • Report at least two things that were actually said between you, using reported speech (statements and/or questions).
  • Explain whether the friendship ended through a conversation or through silence — and why.
  • Reflect on whether you ever got closure, or whether honesty at the time would have changed things.
  • End by saying what you would do differently if it happened again today.
