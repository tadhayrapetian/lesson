Holidays for Sleeping

The newest travel trend isn’t about seeing more. It’s about sleeping better.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • understand an article about a new travel trend
  • talk about future possibilities with if, when, unless and as soon as
  • design and present a hotel idea

Teacher's overview

Main aim: Speaking: to plan together and present a product persuasively.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for gist and detail. Vocabulary for rest and travel.

Background: Sleep tourism is a growing travel trend. Hotels and retreats offer sleep programmes, special bedrooms, phone-free rules and relaxing treatments.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. How many hours do you usually sleep?
  2. What helps you sleep well? What stops you sleeping?
  3. On holiday, do you prefer to rest or to see as much as possible?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Today’s topic is sleep. Discuss the questions with your partner.

Give students 1 minute to think, then pairs discuss. Take two or three ideas from the class. Do not correct language here. This stage is about interest and ideas.

Say: Great ideas. Today’s text has some useful words for this topic. Let’s look at them first.

3

First read

5 min

Read the text quickly. Choose the best summary.

Do Not Disturb

Hotels around the world are offering a new kind of holiday: the sleep trip.

Lucia works in marketing in Madrid. Last year she felt exhausted all the time. ‘I slept five hours a night,’ she says. So for her holiday, she didn’t choose a beach or a city. She booked a sleep retreat in the mountains.

Sleep tourism is one of the fastest-growing travel trends. Hotels now offer special pillows, dark curtains and ‘sleep menus’ with calming teas. Some rooms have smart beds that check your sleep. Guests can book relaxing treatments such as massages and evening yoga.

The rules are simple. Many retreats ask guests to give their phone to reception in the evening. ‘If you look at a screen before bed, your brain will stay awake,’ explains a sleep coach at one hotel. Guests go to bed and wake up at the same time every day.

Does it work? Most guests say yes, at least for a short time. ‘As soon as I arrived, I felt calmer,’ says Lucia. ‘After three nights, I was sleeping eight hours.’ But experts warn that a few nights in a hotel won’t fix a sleep problem unless you change your routine at home.

So, is a sleep holiday worth the money? It is often expensive. But for many tired workers, it’s a chance to recharge. ‘When I get home, I’ll keep my phone out of the bedroom,’ says Lucia. ‘That’s the real gift from this holiday.’

Teacher notes. 5 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Don’t stop at new words. Choose the best summary. You have 3 minutes.

ICQs: “Do you need to understand every word?” (No.) “How many answers do you choose?” (One.)

Set a strict time limit. Pairs compare. In feedback ask: “Which part of the text helped you?”

Answer: B. The whole text is about hotels that help guests sleep. Phones are only one part.

Say: Now read again, more carefully this time.

4

Close read

8 min

Read again. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • Lucia booked a beach holiday last year.
  • Some hotel rooms check how well you sleep.
  • At many retreats, guests keep their phones all evening.
  • Lucia slept better after a few nights.
  • Experts say a sleep holiday alone will solve sleep problems.
  • Lucia plans to change something at home.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read again carefully. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)? Underline the part of the text that gives you the answer.

ICQ: “Do you need to show where the answer is?” (Yes.)

Pairs compare and show each other the evidence. In feedback, always ask “Where does it say that?”

Say: Well done. Now let’s look closely at some language from the text.

5

Language focus

10 min

Look at these sentences from the text.

  • If you look at a screen before bed, your brain will stay awake.
  • ...a few nights in a hotel won’t fix a sleep problem unless you change your routine at home.
  • When I get home, I’ll keep my phone out of the bedroom.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Are these sentences about the past, a real future possibility or an imaginary situation?A real possibility in the future.
  • Which tense comes after if, when and unless?The present simple (look, change, get), even with a future meaning.
  • What does unless mean?if... not. ‘unless you change’ = ‘if you don’t change’.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Focus: first conditional and time clauses.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Work with a partner and answer the questions. Don’t worry about rules yet. Just look for patterns.

Monitor. Then elicit answers and build the rule on the board with the students. Students tell you the rule. You write it.

Rule for the board: If + present simple, will / won’t + base verb = a real future possibility. when = I’m sure it will happen. if = it’s possible. unless = if not. as soon as = immediately when. After if, when, unless and as soon as, use the present simple, not will.

CCQs: ‘When I get home, I’ll call you.’ Am I sure I will get home? (Yes.) ‘If I get home early, I’ll call you.’ Am I sure I’ll be early? (No.)

Common mistake: ‘When I will get home...’ No will after when, if, unless or as soon as.

Say: Now let’s practise it.

6

Practice

7 min

Complete the sentences with the correct form of the verb in brackets.

  • If you drink (drink) coffee at night, you won’t sleep well.
  • I will feel (feel) better tomorrow if I go to bed early.
  • As soon as I finish (finish) this report, I’ll book a holiday.
  • You won’t feel rested unless you turn off (turn off) your phone.
  • When we arrive (arrive) at the hotel, we’ll have a massage.
  • If the room is noisy, I will ask (ask) for a different one.

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Work alone first. Complete the sentences. Then check with your partner.

ICQs: ‘Alone or with a partner first?’ (Alone.) ‘After when or if, do we use will?’ (No.)

Fast finishers write one more sentence of their own with the target language.

In feedback, write the answers on the board. Focus on form errors. Ask “Why this form?” for one or two items.

Say: Perfect. Now you’re ready to use all of this in a real conversation.

7

Speaking

12 min

Design a sleep hotel

You are opening a new sleep hotel. Plan it, give it a name and sell it to the class.

The room

What’s in it? Think about the bed, the light, sounds and smells.

The rules

What can guests do? What can’t they do?

The activities

What do guests do during the day and in the evening?

The promise

How will guests feel when they leave?

Steps

  1. Plan your hotel. Decide on the four points on the cards.
  2. Choose a name and a place.
  3. Present your hotel to the class like an advert. Use if, when and as soon as.
  4. Listen to the other hotels. Choose one to visit and say why.

Useful language

If you stay with us, you’ll...As soon as you arrive, ...You won’t... unless...Guests can... / Guests can’t...It sounds relaxing, but...I’d choose... because...

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs or small groups.

Say: You’re opening a new sleep hotel. Plan it together using the four cards. Give it a name. Then you will sell it to the class.

Pairs or groups of three. 4 min planning, 1 to 2 min presentation each, 2 min vote. In 1:1, you are a guest who asks difficult questions.

Give clear preparation time before students speak. Monitor closely. Write down good language and errors in two columns. Do not interrupt. Fluency comes first here.

One-to-one: you take the other role or position. Keep your turns short and push the student to justify and expand (“Why?”, “Can you give an example?”).

Say: Time’s up. Let’s look at some of the language you used.

8

Reflection and feedback

3 min

Think about today's lesson.

Teacher notes. 3 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board: some correct, some with errors. Do not say who said them.

Say: Here are some sentences I heard. Some are perfect. Some have a mistake. Work with your partner and find the mistakes.

Students correct in pairs. Elicit the corrections. Praise good language and highlight two or three great phrases students used.

Errors students often make with this lesson:

  • “If I will sleep more, I will feel better.” > “If I sleep more, I will feel better.”
  • “I’m very boring at work, so I need a holiday.” > “I’m very bored at work, so I need a holiday.”
  • “I felt myself relaxed.” > “I felt relaxed.”

Then students complete the reflection boxes. Ask one or two students to read theirs aloud.

9

Homework

Set at the end

A. Vocabulary review. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson. Change the form if you need to.

routinerechargeexhaustedguestsrelaxing
  • After the marathon, I was completely exhausted.
  • My morning routine is simple: shower, coffee, a ten-minute walk.
  • The hotel had 200 guests last weekend.
  • A warm bath is very relaxing after a long day.
  • I need a weekend in the countryside to recharge.

B. Writing: a hotel review (110-140 words)

Imagine you stayed at a sleep retreat. Write a review for a travel website. Describe the room, the rules and the activities. Did you sleep better? Would you go again?

Checklist

  • Use the first conditional at least twice.
  • Use four words from the lesson.
  • Give the hotel a score from 1 to 5 and explain it.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and Part B. In Part B, use at least four words from today and the grammar we studied.

Collect the writing next lesson. Mark it for four things: task completion, vocabulary from the lesson, accuracy with the target language, and organisation.

Look for will after when or if. It is the most common error with this structure.

Start the next lesson with a two-minute vocabulary quiz using the words from Part A.
