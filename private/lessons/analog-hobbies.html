The Joy of Doing It Slowly

Film cameras, vinyl records and knitting are back. Why are young people choosing the slow way?

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • match headings to paragraphs to understand the main ideas
  • find specific information and opinions in a longer text
  • guess the meaning of words from context

Warm-up and prediction

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. Look at the title. What do you think ‘doing it slowly’ means?
  2. Which of these have you tried: film cameras, vinyl records, knitting, board games, writing a diary?
  3. Why would a young person choose a slow hobby today?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Look at the title and the subtitle. What do you think the text is about? Discuss the questions.

Write students’ predictions on the board. Check them after the first reading.

3

First read: headings

9 min

The Joy of Doing It Slowly

1Walk into a second-hand shop in any big city and you might see a teenager carefully looking at an old film camera. Twenty years ago, people thought these cameras were rubbish. Now some sell for more than a new phone. Film photography, vinyl records, knitting, board games and handwritten journals are all becoming popular again, especially with people under 30.

2Why? One reason is that digital life never stops. Phones are fast, easy and always with us, but they can also feel tiring. ‘On my phone I take 200 photos and look at none of them,’ says Lea, 22. ‘With film, I have 36 pictures on a roll. I think about every one.’ Analog hobbies make people slow down.

3Another reason is the feeling of owning something real. A playlist can disappear if you stop paying for an app, but a record stays on your shelf. Knitters say they love finishing a scarf that they can touch and wear. ‘It’s proof that I made something,’ explains Sam, 19.

4There is also a social side. Board game cafés are full on Friday nights, and knitting clubs meet in bars and libraries. Many young people say these hobbies help them make friends without screens in the middle.

5Of course, analog hobbies aren’t cheap. A roll of film and developing can cost more than 20 euros, and vinyl records are expensive too. And ironically, many people share their film photos and knitting projects on social media. ‘It’s not about hating technology,’ says Lea. ‘It’s about choosing when to use it.’

Read quickly. Match each paragraph 1-5 to a heading a-e.

  • Paragraph 1b
  • Paragraph 2e
  • Paragraph 3d
  • Paragraph 4c
  • Paragraph 5a
  • aNot perfect, and not against technology
  • bOld things, new fans

  • dSomething real in your hands
  • eSlowing down in a fast world

Teacher notes. 9 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Match each paragraph to a heading. Don’t read every word. You have four minutes.

ICQ: ‘Do you need to understand every word?’ (No.) ‘How many headings for each paragraph?’ (One.)

Strategy tip: read the first and last sentence of each paragraph. Show students how this helps.

Check the predictions from stage 1.

4

Close read

11 min

Read again carefully. Choose the correct answer.

  • What does the writer say about old film cameras?
  • Why does Lea prefer film?
  • What is the problem with a playlist, according to the text?
  • Where do knitting clubs meet?
  • What does Lea mean at the end?

Teacher notes. 11 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Now read again carefully. Choose the correct answer. Underline the part of the text that helps you.

Pairs compare. In feedback, always ask ‘Which line?’

Item 5 is about the writer’s or speaker’s meaning. Discuss why the other two options are wrong.

Words in context

7 min

Find a word in the text that means...

  • old and useless (paragraph 1)rubbish
  • making you feel tired (paragraph 2)tiring
  • a flat board on the wall for books or records (paragraph 3)shelf
  • a long piece of clothing you wear around your neck (paragraph 3)scarf
  • in a way that is the opposite of what you expect (paragraph 5)ironically

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Find these words in the text. The paragraph number helps you.

Teach the strategy: look at the words before and after. What kind of word is it? A noun? An adjective?

6

Speaking: your slow day

15 min

Discuss the questions.

  1. Which analog hobby would you like to try? Why?
  2. Are analog hobbies just a trend, or will they last?
  3. Is it strange to share analog hobbies on social media?
  4. What do you do when you want to slow down?

Now plan a ‘slow Saturday’ for your partner: no screens from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. What will they do?

Useful language

I’d love to try... because...I think it’s just a trend.It helps me slow down.In the morning, you could...That sounds relaxing / boring!What about lunch?

Teacher notes. 15 min. Pairs.

Say: Discuss the questions with your partner. Then plan a slow Saturday for each other. No screens!

8 min discussion, 5 min planning, 2 min sharing the best plans with the class.

One-to-one: plan a slow Saturday for each other and compare.

7

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘I want to try to knit.’ > ‘I want to try knitting.’
  • ‘make photos’ > ‘take photos’
  • ‘It is very tired to use a phone all day.’ > ‘It is very tiring...’
