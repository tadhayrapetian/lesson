Nostalgia & the Weight of Generations

Every generation insists the world was simpler, kinder, or better before it arrived — this lesson asks whether they're actually right, or just remembering selectively.

Warm-up

Lead-in Discussion

Before we look at any language, talk about these questions with a partner.

  • Does nostalgia distort our memory of the past, or does it preserve something real that simply gets lost over time?
  • Is there anything from a decade you never actually lived through that you feel strangely nostalgic for? Why might that happen?
  • What do you think today's teenagers will feel nostalgic for in twenty years — and will older generations even understand it?
  • Is the claim "things were better back then" ever objectively true, or is it always a matter of perspective and selective memory?

Grammar Focus

used to / would vs. Present Simple

Form & Use

used to + base verb — describes a habit or a state that was true in the past but is no longer true. It works for both repeated actions ("I used to smoke") and states ("I used to live in Yerevan", "I used to be shy").

would + base verb — describes a repeated action in the past, often used when reminiscing or telling stories. Crucially, would does NOT work for past states — you cannot say "I would live in Yerevan" or "I would be shy" to describe a past condition. States need used to (or the simple past).

present simple — describes habits, routines, and facts that are true now.

Examples tied to today's topic

  • My grandmother used to walk three miles to school every morning, and she would tell that story at every family dinner.
  • People used to write long letters to relatives abroad; now we simply send a text and expect an instant reply.
  • My father would spend hours fixing the radio himself, but I just take my broken phone straight to the shop.
  • She used to believe that the past was always simpler — until she started reading real history. (state — "would believe" is impossible here)
  • As a boy, my grandfather would sit on the porch for hours after dinner; nowadays, children rarely sit still that long.

Controlled Practice

Exercises

Part A — Multiple choice

1. There ______ a small cinema at the end of our street, but it closed down years ago.

2. My grandfather ______ every evening after dinner, no matter the weather.

3. My mother says she ______ her freedom until she became a parent herself.

4. Nowadays, most families ______ dinner together far less often than in the past.

Part B — Fill in the blank

Write the correct form (used to, would, or present simple) of the verb in brackets. Think carefully about whether the sentence describes a state or a repeated action.

5. My grandparents (not / have) a television until they were almost thirty.

6. Every summer, my great-uncle (tell) the same stories about the war, and nobody ever complained.

7. In many families today, grandparents (live) far from their grandchildren, so video calls have replaced Sunday visits.

8. My father (be) painfully shy as a teenager, though it's hard to imagine now.

Part C — Transformation & error correction

9. Rewrite this sentence with would if it is possible. If it is not possible, explain briefly why in your own words.

"My grandmother used to be the strictest teacher in the village school."

10. Find the mistake in this sentence and correct it.

"When I was small, my parents would own a little shop near the train station."

Production Task

Long Turn: A Generation Apart

Speak for 2–3 minutes on the following topic: "How someone from an older generation in your life used to live — and what that says about nostalgia." Use these points to guide you:

  • Describe how a parent, grandparent, or older relative used to live day-to-day when they were young.
  • Use at least three vocabulary words or phrases from today's lesson.
  • Use both used to and would where appropriate — and be ready to explain why you chose one over the other for at least one state verb.
  • Compare that past lifestyle with one of your own present-day habits, using the present simple.
  • Finish with your own opinion: is nostalgia for that earlier era justified, or is it romanticized?
