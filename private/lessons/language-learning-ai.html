Why Learn a Language Now?

AI can translate almost anything instantly. Write an argumentative essay on whether learning languages still matters.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • structure a C1 argumentative essay with a clear thesis and topic sentences
  • use cohesive devices and hedging for an academic tone
  • plan, write and peer-review an essay

Subsidiary aims: Reading a model essay for argument and structure.

Background: Real-time translation is now built into many phones and earbuds. Research suggests that bilingualism may be associated with some cognitive benefits, although findings vary. The essay format matches C1 exam essays (for example, Cambridge C1 Advanced).

1

Warm-up

7 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. Have you used AI or an app to translate a conversation? How well did it work?
  2. Why are you learning English? Would AI translation change that?
  3. What can a person who speaks a language do that an app can’t?

Teacher notes. 7 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Today you’ll write an essay about a question that affects all of us as language learners. First, discuss these questions.

Collect ideas on the board in two columns: for and against learning languages in the age of AI. Students will use them later.

2

Read the model

12 min

Is language learning still worth it in the age of AI?

1Real-time translation, once the stuff of science fiction, is now available on almost every smartphone. Earbuds can translate a conversation as it happens, and AI tools can produce fluent versions of entire documents in seconds. It is therefore tempting to conclude that learning a foreign language has become an expensive hobby. This essay argues, however, that the value of language learning lies precisely in what machines cannot do.

2Admittedly, for practical tasks such as reading a menu, booking a hotel or understanding a contract, translation tools are remarkably effective, and they will almost certainly improve further. For millions of travellers and businesses, this represents a genuine advance, and it would be unrealistic to deny it.

3Nevertheless, communication is far more than the transfer of information. Relationships, whether personal or professional, depend on humour, trust and the ability to read what is left unsaid. A person who has made the effort to learn a language signals respect, and is able to respond in the moment, without a device mediating every exchange. It is difficult to imagine a friendship, let alone a marriage, conducted entirely through an app.

4Furthermore, learning a language changes the learner. Research suggests that bilingualism may be associated with cognitive benefits, such as greater mental flexibility, and studying a language tends to deepen understanding of other cultures. These benefits arise from the process of learning itself, which no translation tool can complete on our behalf.

5In conclusion, while AI translation is a valuable tool for everyday transactions, it is no substitute for the connection, insight and personal growth that come from learning a language. If anything, as machines take over routine translation, the human ability to communicate directly may become more valuable, not less.

What is the writer’s position?

  • The writer believes that...

Teacher notes. 12 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the model essay. What is the writer’s position? Do you agree?

Discuss briefly. Then move to analysis: ‘Why is this a good C1 essay?’

3

Structure

10 min

Match each paragraph to its function.

  • Paragraph 1b
  • Paragraph 2a
  • Paragraph 3e
  • Paragraph 4c
  • Paragraph 5d
  • aConcession: the strongest opposing point
  • bIntroduction: context and thesis statement
  • cSecond main argument: benefits to the learner
  • dConclusion: summary and a final, forward-looking thought
  • eFirst main argument: communication and relationships

Find the thesis statement and copy it.

  • The thesis statement is:This essay argues, however, that the value of language learning lies precisely in what machines cannot do.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs.

Say: Match each paragraph to its function. Then find the one sentence that states the writer’s position.

Highlight: the concession comes before the main arguments. This makes the essay more persuasive and balanced.

Point out the topic sentence at the start of each body paragraph.

Cohesion

12 min

Complete the sentences with the words in the box. Use each one once.

On balanceMoreoverHoweverAdmittedlytherefore
  • AI translation is fast. However, it often misses cultural nuance.
  • Admittedly, apps are useful for travellers; few would deny this.
  • Bilingual people may find it easier to switch between tasks. Moreover, they often understand other cultures better.
  • Translation tools are improving; therefore, demand for basic human translation is likely to fall.
  • On balance, the advantages of learning a language outweigh the costs.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: First, sort the linkers by function. Then use them in new sentences.

Point out punctuation: ‘However,’ at the start of a sentence; ‘; therefore,’ in the middle.

Warn against overuse. One linker per sentence, at most. Good essays also link ideas through reference words and repetition of key nouns.

5

Hedging

10 min

Academic writing avoids claims that are too strong. Compare: ‘Bilingual people are smarter.’ and ‘Bilingualism may be associated with cognitive benefits.’

Rewrite each claim in a more cautious, academic way. Use the word given.

  • Bilingual people are smarter.associatedBilingualism is associated with certain cognitive benefits.
  • AI will replace all translators.likelyAI is likely to replace some translation work.
  • Learning a language makes you understand other cultures.tendsLearning a language tends to deepen understanding of other cultures.
  • Everyone hates using translation apps in conversation.manyMany people find translation apps frustrating in conversation.
  • This proves that languages are still important.suggestsThis suggests that languages remain important.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Make these claims more careful. Use the word given.

Accept any well-hedged version. Explain why overclaiming weakens an essay: examiners and readers stop trusting you.

6

Plan

9 min

Question A

‘Universities should stop requiring students to study a foreign language.’ Discuss.

Question B

‘In the age of AI, the most important language skill is knowing how to use translation tools.’ To what extent do you agree?

Question C

‘Smaller languages, such as Armenian, will gain more than they lose from AI translation.’ Discuss.

Teacher notes. 9 min. Individual.

Say: Choose one question. Plan your essay using the model structure. Write your thesis as a full sentence. Everything else can be notes.

Check each student’s thesis before they start writing. A clear thesis makes the whole essay easier.

7

Write

22 min

Write your essay (280-320 words).

Write your essay. Follow your plan and the model structure.

Checklist

  • A clear thesis statement in the introduction.
  • A topic sentence at the start of each body paragraph.
  • A fair concession, answered convincingly.
  • At least five linkers and three hedging expressions.
  • A conclusion with a final, forward-looking thought.

Teacher notes. 22 min. Individual.

Say: Write your essay. You have twenty-two minutes. Use your plan and the checklist.

Exam-like conditions. Give a five-minute warning.

8

Peer review

8 min

Swap essays with your partner. Read and answer:

  1. Is the thesis clear in the introduction?
  2. Does each body paragraph have a clear topic sentence?
  3. Is there a fair concession, and is it answered?
  4. Find three linkers and two hedging expressions.
  5. What is the strongest sentence in the essay? Why?

Teacher notes. 8 min. Pairs.

Say: Swap essays. Read your partner’s essay and answer the questions. Write two comments: one strength, one improvement.

Model useful feedback: ‘Your concession is fair, but the answer to it could be stronger. Maybe add an example.’

9

Homework

Set at the end

Write your final draft (280-320 words).

Rewrite your essay using your partner’s comments. Then add a one-sentence summary of your argument at the top, as if for a class blog.

Checklist

  • Improve at least two sentences your partner commented on.
  • Check articles, prepositions and verb forms.
  • Make your conclusion memorable.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, write your final draft and the one-sentence summary.

Mark against C1 criteria: content, communicative achievement, organisation and language. Give one ‘keep doing’ and one ‘next step’.
