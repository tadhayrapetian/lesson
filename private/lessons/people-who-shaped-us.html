    The People Who Made Us

    Three short stories about influence

    Ask anyone who shaped the way they see the world, and the answer is rarely a celebrity. More often it is a grandmother, a teacher or a stranger who said the right thing at the right moment. We asked three people to tell their stories.

    Anna

    34, architect

    "My grandmother lived in a tiny village, but she was the most open-minded person I knew. She would read three newspapers every morning and then argue with all of them. She used to say, 'Don't believe something just because it's printed.' She instilled a love of questions in me. When I travel now, I still hear her voice. She broadened my horizons without ever leaving her village."

    David

    41, nurse

    "At school I was the boy at the back who never tried. Then Mr Petrosyan, our history teacher, gave me an essay back with one line on it: 'You clearly think for yourself. Now think harder.' Nobody had expected anything from me before. That was a turning point. I still look up to him, and I try to do the same for the young nurses I train."

    Lilit

    27, software developer

    "The person who had the biggest impact on me was a stranger. I was 19 and stuck at a train station in Italy with no money and a dead phone. An old woman bought me a coffee and a ticket and refused to take my number. She said, 'Just do the same for someone else one day.' That moment challenged my assumptions about strangers. I used to think people only helped when it was easy for them. Now I don't take kindness for granted, and I try to pass it on."

    None of these people planned to be a role model. They were honest, patient or kind at the right time. That is the encouraging part. Each of us may already be shaping someone's view of the world without knowing it.

      Grammar: used to and would

      8 min

      From the text

      She would read three newspapers every morning.

      I used to think people only helped when it was easy.

      Both talk about the past that is not true now.

      used to works for repeated actions and for states: I used to live in Yerevan. I used to be shy.
      would works only for repeated actions: We would go fishing every summer.
      Not: I would be shy.

      Choose

      Which form is correct? Choose used to, would or both.

      Speaking

      12 min

      Part A. Discuss

      • Can a person you have never met be your role model?
      • Do parents shape us more than friends? Why or why not?
      • What belief did you have as a child that someone later challenged?
      • Who in your life do you take for granted? How could you show them you value them?

      Part B. Tell the story

      Choose one person who shaped the way you see the world. Prepare for one minute, then talk for two minutes. Use the prompts.

      1. Who is this person, and how do you know them?
      2. What did they use to do or say?
      3. Describe one moment you remember clearly.
      4. How did they change the way you think or act?
      5. What would you say to them today?

      Useful language

      The person who had the biggest impact on me is ...I've always looked up to ...What I admire most is ...It was a real turning point because ...Thanks to them, I ...Looking back, I realise ...If I could tell them one thing, it would be ...
