The Kitchen Nobody Uses

Food delivery apps have changed how we eat. Are we forgetting how to cook?

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • understand an article about food delivery
  • talk about amounts with too much, too many, enough, a few and a little
  • debate a habit and plan a healthy, affordable week of meals

Teacher's overview

Length: 60 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Speaking: to debate a lifestyle habit and plan a compromise.

Subsidiary aims: Reading for gist and detail. Vocabulary for food and services.

Language aim: Quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns.

1

Warm-up

5 min

Talk with a partner.

  1. How often do you order food to your home?
  2. What is your favourite thing to order? What is your favourite thing to cook?
  3. Is it cheaper to cook or to order where you live?

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class, then pairs.

Say: Let’s talk about food. Discuss these questions with your partner.

Give students 1 minute to think, then pairs discuss. Take two or three ideas from the class. Do not correct language here. This stage is about interest and ideas.

Say: Great ideas. Today’s text has some useful words for this topic. Let’s look at them first.

3

First read

5 min

Read the text quickly. Choose the best summary.

The Kitchen Nobody Uses

More people order dinner on their phones every week. What does this mean for our health, our money and our kitchens?

It’s 8 p.m. You’re tired after work and the fridge is almost empty. You open an app, choose a burger and order it in ten seconds. Twenty minutes later, a courier is at your door. For millions of people, this is a normal evening.

Food delivery has grown fast in the last ten years, especially since 2020. In big cities, you can get almost anything: sushi, khachapuri, coffee, even one bottle of water. The main reason is simple. It is very convenient.

But there are problems. First, the price. A meal from an app costs a lot more than the same meal at home, because of delivery fees and service charges. ‘I spent too much money on delivery last month,’ says Arman, 24. ‘I looked at my bank app and couldn’t believe it.’

Health is another worry. Many popular dishes have too much salt, sugar and fat, and the portions are big. Nutritionists say that people who order often don’t eat enough vegetables. There is also the packaging. One dinner can come with a lot of plastic boxes and bags.

Some people are trying to change the habit. Meal-kit companies send boxes with all the ingredients and a simple recipe. Others cook a few big dishes on Sunday for the whole week. ‘It takes a little time,’ says Lena, 30, ‘but a home-cooked meal tastes better, and I know what’s in it.’

Teacher notes. 5 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text quickly. Don’t stop at new words. Choose the best summary. You have 3 minutes.

ICQs: “Do you need to understand every word?” (No.) “How many answers do you choose?” (One.)

Set a strict time limit. Pairs compare. In feedback ask: “Which part of the text helped you?”

Answer: A. The text explains why delivery is popular, the costs, and how some people are changing.

Say: Now read again, more carefully this time.

4

Close read

8 min

Read again. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • The courier arrives in about twenty minutes.
  • Food delivery started for the first time in 2020.
  • A meal from an app costs about the same as a meal at home.
  • Arman was surprised by how much he spent.
  • People who order often eat a lot of vegetables.
  • Lena likes to know what is in her food.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read again carefully. Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)? Underline the part of the text that gives you the answer.

ICQ: “Do you need to show where the answer is?” (Yes.)

Pairs compare and show each other the evidence. In feedback, always ask “Where does it say that?”

Say: Well done. Now let’s look closely at some language from the text.

5

Language focus

10 min

Look at these sentences from the text.

  • I spent too much money on delivery.
  • Nutritionists say that people who order often don’t eat enough vegetables.
  • One dinner can come with a lot of plastic boxes.
  • Others cook a few big dishes on Sunday.
  • It takes a little time.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Which phrase means ‘more than you want or need’?too much (and too many)
  • Do we say too much money or too many money? Why?too much money. money is uncountable.
  • Does enough go before or after a noun?Before a noun: enough vegetables. After an adjective: big enough.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Focus: quantifiers: too much, too many, enough, a few, a little, a lot of.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Work with a partner and answer the questions. Don’t worry about rules yet. Just look for patterns.

Monitor. Then elicit answers and build the rule on the board with the students. Students tell you the rule. You write it.

Rule for the board: Countable nouns: too many, a few, (not) enough, a lot of. Uncountable nouns: too much, a little, (not) enough, a lot of. too much / too many = more than is good. not enough = less than you need. enough + noun. adjective + enough.

CCQs: ‘There’s a little sugar.’ Is there some sugar? (Yes.) A lot? (No.) ‘There’s too much sugar.’ Is that good? (No.)

Students often say ‘too much people’. people is countable: too many people.

Say: Now let’s practise it.

7 min

ICQs: ‘Alone or with a partner first?’ (Alone.) ‘Is money countable or uncountable?’ (Uncountable.)

Fast finishers write one more sentence of their own with the target language.

In feedback, write the answers on the board. Focus on form errors. Ask “Why this form?” for one or two items.

Say: Perfect. Now you’re ready to use all of this in a real conversation.

7

Speaking

12 min

The delivery debate

Two flatmates can’t agree. One loves ordering food. The other loves cooking. Debate, then plan a fair week of dinners.

Flatmate A

You work until 8 p.m. You are too tired to cook. Delivery saves you time, and you like trying new food.

Flatmate B

You love cooking. You think delivery is expensive, unhealthy and bad for the planet.

The plan

Seven dinners. How many will you order? How many will you cook? What will you make?

Steps

  1. Choose a flatmate card. Add two more reasons of your own.
  2. Debate: is ordering food a good or a bad habit?
  3. Now work together. Plan seven dinners for next week.
  4. Present your plan. Explain why it’s fair.

Useful language

It’s too expensive to... every day.I don’t have enough time to...We could order... once a week.That’s a good point, but...How about cooking... on Sunday?Let’s meet in the middle.

Teacher notes. 12 min. Pairs or small groups.

Say: Two flatmates can’t agree about food. Choose a card. Think of two more reasons. Then debate with your partner.

Pairs. 2 min preparation, 4 min debate, 5 min planning, 1 min each to present. Make sure the debate part stays short and the planning part is collaborative.

Give clear preparation time before students speak. Monitor closely. Write down good language and errors in two columns. Do not interrupt. Fluency comes first here.

One-to-one: you take the other role or position. Keep your turns short and push the student to justify and expand (“Why?”, “Can you give an example?”).

Say: Time’s up. Let’s look at some of the language you used.

8

Reflection and feedback

3 min

Think about today's lesson.

Teacher notes. 3 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board: some correct, some with errors. Do not say who said them.

Say: Here are some sentences I heard. Some are perfect. Some have a mistake. Work with your partner and find the mistakes.

Students correct in pairs. Elicit the corrections. Praise good language and highlight two or three great phrases students used.

Errors students often make with this lesson:

  • “There are too much plastic boxes.” > “There are too many plastic boxes.”
  • “I don’t have time enough.” > “I don’t have enough time.”
  • “The delivery app is very comfortable.” > “The delivery app is very convenient.”

Then students complete the reflection boxes. Ask one or two students to read theirs aloud.

9

Homework

Set at the end

A. Vocabulary review. Complete the sentences with words from the lesson. Change the form if you need to.

habitcourierfeeconvenientingredients
  • You need only four ingredients for this cake: flour, eggs, sugar and butter.
  • Eating late at night is a bad habit.
  • The shop is open 24 hours, which is very convenient.
  • The courier brought my parcel to the wrong flat.
  • There is a small delivery fee of 500 drams.

B. Writing: a short review (100-130 words)

Write a short review of a café, restaurant or delivery app you know. Describe the food, the price and the service. Would you recommend it?

Checklist

  • Use at least four quantifiers, for example too much, enough, a few.
  • Use four words from the lesson.
  • Give a clear recommendation at the end.

Teacher notes. Set at the end, 2 min. Individual.

Say: For homework, do Part A and Part B. In Part B, use at least four words from today and the grammar we studied.

Collect the writing next lesson. Mark it for four things: task completion, vocabulary from the lesson, accuracy with the target language, and organisation.

comfortable for convenient is a very common error for Russian and Armenian speakers. Check for it.

Start the next lesson with a two-minute vocabulary quiz using the words from Part A.
