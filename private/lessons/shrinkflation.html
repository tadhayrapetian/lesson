Same Price, Smaller Bag

Products are shrinking, but prices aren’t. Learn to compare like a price detective.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • compare things with comparatives, superlatives and as... as
  • make comparisons stronger or weaker with much, far, slightly and a bit
  • talk about prices and changes in the shops

Background: ‘Shrinkflation’ means making products smaller while keeping the same price. Since July 2024, supermarkets in France must put labels on products that have shrunk. The lesson follows a Test-Teach-Test structure.

3

Read

8 min

Same Price, Less Product

Have you noticed that your favourite crisps disappear faster than before? You are not imagining it.

Many companies now sell smaller products at the same price. This is called ‘shrinkflation’. A bag of crisps that weighed 200 grams can become 180 grams. The bag looks almost as big as before, so most people don’t notice.

Companies say their costs are much higher than a few years ago, because energy, transport and ingredients cost more. They say making products a bit smaller is better than raising prices.

But shoppers are angry. ‘I feel tricked,’ says Marta, a mother of three. ‘When the price is higher, I can see it. A smaller packet is far harder to notice.’ In France, supermarkets must now put labels on products that have become smaller.

The best advice? Check the price per kilo, not the price per packet.

Are the sentences true (T) or false (F)?

  • Shrinkflation means products get smaller but cost the same.
  • The new crisp bag looks much smaller than the old one.
  • Companies say their costs have gone up.
  • Marta says smaller packets are easy to notice.
  • French supermarkets must label products that have become smaller.
  • The writer says you should compare the price per packet.

Teacher notes. 8 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Read the text. Are the sentences true or false? You have four minutes.

ICQ: ‘Do you need to show where the answer is in the text?’ (Yes.)

Pairs compare. In feedback ask ‘Where does it say that?’

Say: Good. Now look at how the writer compares things.

4

Language focus

10 min

Look at these examples.

  • Their costs are much higher than a few years ago.
  • The bag looks almost as big as before.
  • Making products a bit smaller is better than raising prices.
  • A smaller packet is far harder to notice.
  • The best advice? Check the price per kilo.

Answer the questions with a partner.

  • Which words make a comparison stronger? Which make it weaker?Stronger: much, far, a lot. Weaker: a bit, slightly, a little.
  • How do we compare two things that are almost the same?almost as + adjective + as. Also: nearly as... as, not quite as... as.
  • How do we form comparatives of short and long adjectives? What about good and bad?Short: -er (higher, harder). Long: more + adjective (more expensive). good, better, best. bad, worse, worst.

Rule for the board: comparatives, superlatives and modifiers

Comparative: adjective + -er / more + adjective + than. Superlative: the + -est / the most + adjective. Big difference: much / far / a lot + comparative. Small difference: a bit / slightly / a little + comparative. Equal: as + adjective + as. Almost equal: almost / nearly as... as. Not equal: not as... as.

CCQs: ‘Coffee is slightly more expensive.’ Big change or small change? (Small.) ‘Coffee is far more expensive.’ (Big.)

Common mistakes: ‘more cheaper’, ‘very more expensive’ (use much) and ‘as bigger as’.

Teacher notes. 10 min. Pairs, then whole class.

Say: Look at the sentences from the text. Answer the questions with your partner.

Elicit the rule. Draw a scale on the board: slightly / a bit on one side, much / far / a lot on the other.

Say: Now go back to stage 2. Check your answers and change them if you need to.

Students self-correct the test. Confirm the answers from the key.

5

Practice: forms

7 min

Complete the sentences with the correct form of the adjective in brackets.

  • Supermarket milk is cheaper (cheap) than milk from the farm shop.
  • This is the best (good) price I’ve seen all week.
  • Prices in the city centre are much more expensive (expensive) than here.
  • The new bottle isn’t as big (big) as the old one.
  • Online shopping is a bit easier (easy) than going to the shop.
  • That was the worst (bad) coffee I’ve ever had!

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Work alone. Complete the sentences. Then check with your partner.

ICQ: ‘Look at the words around the gap. Do you see than, the or as?’ (Yes. They help you.)

Write answers on the board. Ask ‘Why more expensive, not expensiver?’ for item 3.

6

Practice: modifiers

7 min

Read the facts. Write one comparison with the word given.

  • Bread was 500 drams. Now it is 520 drams.slightlyBread is slightly more expensive now.
  • The old box had 500 g. The new box has 300 g.muchThe new box is much smaller than the old one.
  • Brand A costs 900 drams. Brand B costs 900 drams too.asBrand A is as expensive as Brand B.
  • The old packet was 200 g. The new one is 190 g.nearlyThe new packet is nearly as big as the old one.
  • Petrol is 30% more expensive than last year.a lotPetrol is a lot more expensive than last year.
  • The small shop is cheaper than the supermarket.not asThe supermarket is not as cheap as the small shop.

Teacher notes. 7 min. Individual, then pairs.

Say: Look at the facts. Write one comparison with the word given. Think: is the difference big or small?

Monitor for ‘more cheaper’ and ‘very more’. Accept any correct answer with the right meaning.

7

Speaking: the price detective

13 min

Four products changed between 2023 and today. Compare them and decide which change is the worst.

Chocolate bar

2023: 100 g, 450 drams. Now: 90 g, 450 drams.

Shampoo

2023: 400 ml, 1,800 drams. Now: 350 ml, 1,900 drams.

Coffee

2023: 250 g, 2,500 drams. Now: 250 g, 3,100 drams.

Cereal

2023: 500 g, 1,600 drams. Now: 375 g, 1,600 drams.

Steps

  1. Compare each product then and now. Use much, slightly, far and as... as.
  2. Decide which product changed the most. Is it shrinkflation, a price rise or both?
  3. Discuss: which is worse, a smaller packet or a higher price? Why?
  4. Tell your partner about a product you buy that has changed.

Useful language

The new packet is much smaller.It’s slightly more expensive.It isn’t as big as before.The biggest change is...A higher price is easier to see than...I think shrinkflation is worse because...

Teacher notes. 13 min. Pairs.

Say: You’re price detectives. Look at the four products. What changed? Compare them with your partner.

Pairs. 2 min reading, 6 min comparing, 5 min discussion.

Price per gram rose about 11% for chocolate, 21% for shampoo, 24% for coffee and 33% for cereal. Cereal changed the most.

One-to-one: you compare too, and sometimes make a deliberate mistake for the student to correct.

8

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘more cheaper’ > ‘cheaper’
  • ‘very more expensive’ > ‘much more expensive’
  • ‘as bigger as’ > ‘as big as’
  • ‘the most good’ > ‘the best’
