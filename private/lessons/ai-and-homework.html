Who Wrote This Essay?

AI can do homework in seconds. A student, a teacher, a parent and a researcher disagree about what that means.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • match opinions to people in a multiple-matching task
  • recognise paraphrase between questions and texts
  • discuss and agree on rules for using AI in education

Teacher’s overview

Lesson type: Reading. Length: 75 minutes plus homework.

Main aim: Reading: multiple matching (four short texts), recognising paraphrase.

Subsidiary aims: Speaking: designing a school AI policy. Vocabulary for education and technology.

Language aim: Paraphrase and idiomatic phrases: a blank page, lose patience, an equaliser, face to face.

Background: Generative AI is widely used by students. Some studies suggest students who use AI to get answers can perform worse in tests later, while AI designed as a tutor can support learning. Many schools are still developing policies.

3

First read

9 min

AI and Homework: Four Views

ALucas, 17, student. Honestly, everyone in my class uses AI. Not to copy, mostly, but to get started. When I stare at a blank page, I ask a chatbot for ideas, then I write it myself. Is that cheating? I don’t think so. It’s like asking a friend. The problem is that some teachers ban it completely, so students just hide it. I’d rather schools taught us how to use it properly. In my future job, I’ll be using AI every day, so why pretend it doesn’t exist?

BMs Ortega, English teacher. Last term I received thirty essays that were almost perfect and almost identical. Same structure, same polite conclusions, no mistakes and no personality. Detection tools are unreliable, so I’ve changed my approach. Now my students write first drafts by hand in class, and we discuss their ideas face to face. It takes more time, but I can see how they think. I’m not against technology. I’m against students missing the struggle, because the struggle is where learning happens.

CDavid, parent. My daughter uses AI as a tutor, and it’s amazing. It explains maths problems step by step, as many times as she needs, and it never loses patience, unlike me! We can’t afford a private tutor, so for us it’s a real equaliser. Of course, I check that she isn’t just copying answers. Every Sunday she explains one topic to me without her laptop. If she can’t, we know something’s wrong.

DDr Amara Osei, education researcher. The evidence so far is mixed. Some studies show that students who use AI to get answers do worse in tests later, because they practise less. Others find that AI used as a tutor, asking questions rather than giving answers, can improve learning. So the tool itself isn’t good or bad. What matters is the design and the rules around it. Schools need clear policies, and those policies need to be built with students, not just for them.

Read quickly and answer.

  • Who is most worried about how students write essays?
  • Who talks about research evidence?

Teacher notes. 9 min. Individual.

Say: Read the four texts quickly. Answer two questions. You have four minutes.

Ask: ‘Which person do you agree with most?’ Quick show of hands.

6

Speaking: the school AI policy

18 min

Your group is writing the AI policy for a school. For each task, decide: allowed, allowed if the student says how they used AI, or not allowed.

Brainstorming

Getting ideas for an essay.

Grammar check

Correcting spelling and grammar.

The full essay

Writing the whole essay.

Maths help

Explaining a maths problem step by step.

Translation

Translating homework into English.

Images

Creating pictures for a presentation.

Steps

  1. Discuss each task and decide.
  2. Think about the four views from the texts. Who would agree with your decision?
  3. Write your policy in five short rules.
  4. Present it. Other groups find one weakness in your policy.

Useful language

It’s fine as long as...The danger is that students miss...It could be a real equaliser.Ms Ortega would probably say...How would teachers check?Let’s draw the line at...

Teacher notes. 18 min. Groups of three or four.

Say: You’re writing your school’s AI policy. Decide about each task. Then write five rules.

Groups. 3 min reading, 10 min deciding and writing, 5 min presenting and challenging.

One-to-one: the student writes the policy; you play a teacher, then a student, and react to it.

7

Reflection and feedback

5 min

Think about today’s lesson.

Teacher notes. 5 min. Whole class.

Delayed error correction. Write four or five sentences you heard on the board, some correct and some with errors. Students correct them in pairs. Then they complete the reflection boxes.

Common errors in this lesson:

  • ‘AI can make my homework.’ > ‘AI can do my homework.’
  • ‘It’s a cheating.’ > ‘It’s cheating.’
  • ‘I agree with Lucas opinion.’ > ‘I agree with Lucas’s opinion.’
